# Otis Audagar

*Lives in: Characters → Supporting Cast (or Antagonists — he is not hostile to the protagonists, but his allegiances are entirely his own). Companion to Economy and Enforcement (cold wallets, running cold, the off-grid economy), Water & Food (seed black market, Jennifer Mosswood's chain), Azure Branch (technical knowledge black market), The Margin (adjacent networks, possible contact points), and Aran Sunderwood (first Arbour contact, Act Two B onward).*

---

## What He Is

Otis Audagar is a broker. He does not own the cold wallet infrastructure, the seed black market, the technical knowledge trade, or the shadow supply network that moves goods between the Sprawl, the shadow settlements, and the Badlands communities. He owns none of it. That is precisely the point.

What he owns is information — who has what, who needs what, who can be trusted with an introduction and who cannot, what each party in any given transaction has done before and what it cost them, and what everyone would prefer stayed unrecorded. Over decades of brokering introductions between parties who would not otherwise have found each other, he has accumulated a file on the city's informal economy that is more complete and more accurate than anything Obsidian Branch holds. Not because he set out to build leverage, but because leverage is the natural byproduct of knowing everything that passes through you.

He is cordial. He is genuinely useful. He is not someone to cross, and everyone who has dealt with him for more than five minutes understands this without being told. The understanding is part of the service.

---

## Background

Mustelid — compact, quick-eyed, the kind of build that reads as unremarkable until someone is looking for it specifically. Meridian tier, mid-range habitation, the kind of address that is neither invisible nor conspicuous. He has lived at variations of the same address for longer than most of his current clients have been alive. This is deliberate. Otis Audagar is findable. Findable is part of the offer.

He has been doing this for decades. The network he works through now is not the one he started with — the city changes, networks collapse and reform, people die or get posted or disappear into the fifth division's quiet accounting — but the position he occupies has been his long enough that it has shaped around him the way a room shapes around furniture that has always been there. People don't remember a time when there wasn't someone who knew Otis.

Whether he built his position from nothing or inherited it from a predecessor is a question he has never answered directly. Both versions circulate. He does not correct either.

## How He Operates

Otis does not approach clients. Clients approach him, through channels that are themselves part of the service — introductions through people who already know him, referrals that require the person making them to vouch for the new party. This means every approach carries its own credential: whoever sent you has already put something on the line by doing so.

When he makes an introduction, both parties pay. Not always in Cyph — sometimes in information, sometimes in future favours, sometimes in the simple fact of now being in Otis's record as having conducted a specific transaction. The payment structure varies by what he needs and what the parties have. He is flexible. Flexibility is also part of the service.

His leverage is not threat. He has never, to anyone's knowledge, walked into a room and told someone he would expose them if they didn't comply. What he has done, on occasions where someone has tried to cut him out or burn a relationship he brokered, is quietly make certain information available to certain parties who found it useful. The result is instructive without requiring him to announce what he did. The people who understand how Otis works understand what happened. The people who don't understand yet learn.

## The Networks He Moves Between

Otis does not belong to any of these networks. He is adjacent to all of them.

**The cold wallet infrastructure** — the running cold system that moves Cyph off ARC's registry. Otis controls significant portions of access to this, not because he built the infrastructure but because he knows who did, who maintains it, and who can be trusted to set up a wallet without reporting the transaction. New cold wallets are not something you set up yourself. They require an introduction, and introductions require Otis or someone who knows Otis.

**The technical knowledge black market** — schematics, reclassified failure reports from Azure Branch's Deviation Log (where they leak at all), and the names of people in the Sprawl who understand things the Branch doesn't want understood. Otis sits at the crossroads of this market because the sellers need buyers and the buyers need assurance that what they're purchasing is real and that the seller isn't an Obsidian plant.

**The seed and food network** — the black market for cultivars and genetic material, connected to hidden gardens in the Sprawl and to the Wayfarer trade chain that occasionally brings varieties from the Free Territories. Jennifer Mosswood is one end of a chain that Otis touches further up. He doesn't control Mosswood's operation and doesn't try to — she's been there longer than he has in her specific domain and they maintain a careful, mutually respectful distance. They have sent each other business.

**The shadow settlements** — the communities between Arbour's outer walls and the deeper Badlands, technically outside the Council's jurisdiction and practically dependent on off-grid trade with both the Sprawl and the wider Badlands. Otis has informants here. He also has relationships with specific figures in the settlements who function as his outer edge — the people who notice what arrives from outside Arbour before it reaches the city itself.

**The Margin** — a working arrangement, not an alliance. Otis and Tynan Auberone operate in the same grey-zone substrate — the shared ecosystem of people who move through Arbour's unofficial infrastructure and have developed, over time, the same instincts about when information can safely move. This overlap is what makes the arrangement natural: they are not reaching across a divide, they are acknowledging a shared water.

In practice: Otis has facilitated introductions that benefited The Margin (cold wallet access for harboured individuals, shadow settlement passage, the occasional document that moved through his brokerage before Tynan's people could have reached it). Tynan provides, in return, a read on where Obsidian Branch's attention is concentrated — derived from two decades of watching which cells get broken and when. This is useful to a broker who prefers not to be noticed. Neither side names what they are doing as cooperation. The arrangement operates mostly through one remove, without the principals being identified. Otis has declined some Margin requests without explanation; his calculation — cost versus value — is his own and he does not feel it requires an account.

## Aran

Aran Sunderwood arrives on Arbour's continent without an ARC record, without a registered identity, and without a cold wallet. He is, from Arbour's formal systems perspective, a ghost. From Otis's perspective, he is an unusual and potentially valuable quantity who has just walked out of the ocean and into a shadow settlement where Otis has an informant.

The informant flags him not because Aran does anything wrong, but because a Wayfarer scout — visibly Badlands-marked, asking careful questions, not there to trade — is exactly the kind of anomaly Otis has trained his network to notice.

Otis does not wait for Aran to find him. He finds Aran first.

### The First Meeting

Otis's opening move is a demonstration rather than a conversation. He knows Aran arrived from the Free Territories. He knows approximately when. He knows who Aran spoke to in the settlement and what questions he asked. He is not threatening Aran with this — he is showing him what the information landscape looks like and making clear that Aran is already in it, whether he wanted to be or not.

The offer is straightforward: a cold wallet so Aran can transact without creating an ARC record, and a fabricated identity slotted into the registry so Aran can move through Arbour without being immediately legible as an unregistered outsider. These are the two things Aran needs to function in the city, and Otis can provide both.

The price is information. Specifically: what Aran saw crossing the ocean. What the Badlands communities on the western coast looked like. What the Wayfarer network's current shape is, which routes are still active, which contacts are still reliable. Ground-level intelligence from someone who has just moved through territory that Otis has no current eyes on.

Aran is not being asked to betray anyone. Otis is careful to frame this accurately — he wants trade intelligence, not names of people to harm. Whether Aran believes that framing is his own calculation to make. The exchange is real regardless.

The relationship that develops from this first meeting is professional and wary on both sides. Otis does not become Aran's ally. He becomes Aran's fixer — the person who can get things done in Arbour that cannot be done through official channels, at a price that is always clearly stated and always involves Otis knowing slightly more about Aran than he did before.
