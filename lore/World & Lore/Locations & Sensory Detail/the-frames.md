# The Frames

*Lives in: World & Lore → Locations & Sensory Detail. Companion to Arbour City Geography (position relative to the hull and Sprawl), Azure Branch (founding infrastructure, access tunnel, suppressed audit history), Verdant Branch (the blocked calibration audits), Political Systems (the Twelve, Cassan Vale's tenure), and the Three Act Structure Overview (Act Three climax location). The Frames are the Act Three location for Book One — their full significance should not appear in any in-world document accessible to characters before that point.*

---

## What They Are

The Frames are Arbour's solar collection arrays — large-scale infrastructure built by the founding generation to harvest Cordis's twin-sun output and relay power back to the colony. They sit at a distance from Arbour sufficient to relay power to an established settlement but close enough that on a clear day their silhouette is visible from the Sprawl: a row of dark shapes on the horizon, identifiable as constructed rather than geological, present in the daily visual landscape of the city's lowest tier without ever being accessible to it.

They were erected. They were never properly calibrated. The engineers who understood the calibration systems died before the work was completed — part of the same institutional forgetting that buried Bren Castellan's questions about the solar condenser systems — and the knowledge required to bring them to full output has not been reconstructed in three centuries. They run at a fraction of their designed capacity. Several arrays are net negative, consuming more power than they produce.

The official explanation for restricted access is safety: miscalibrated infrastructure, unstable power outputs, terrain hazards, no personnel currently assigned to the site. Residents of the Sprawl who can see the Frames' silhouette on a good day have always understood, at some level, that the power those arrays should be producing is not reaching them. The official explanation does not address this. It is not designed to.

## Geography

The Frames sit on open terrain outside Arbour's walls — not Badlands in the deep sense, but transitional ground between the city's outer boundary and the wider Cordis landscape. Exposed to the full Cordis atmosphere, unshielded by the hull's atmospheric processing, the site experiences the planet's actual Aetheris concentration rather than the managed, imperfect filtration that Arbour's interior receives.

The Aetheris concentration at the Frames is measurably higher than anywhere within the city. This is not coincidental. The founding generation sited the arrays here deliberately, though their records of why have not survived in any form accessible to current Azure Branch knowledge. The site sits at a point where the boundary between this reality and the Convergence is thin — not an Installation in the Penumbran sense, but a natural weak point in the same geography that produced the Installation cluster near Cassan Vale's childhood settlement. The Frames were built where they were because the ground there responds to energy differently. The founding engineers understood the effect without understanding the cause.

The access tunnel connects the underground facility to ARBOUR|05's hull through terrain that predates the city built around it. It does not appear on any current Azure Branch map. Its existence is documented in founding generation engineering records that have never been cross-referenced with current infrastructure surveys — the kind of document that exists in the Tabularium's restricted floors, misfiled under a reference number that only resolves if you already know what you're looking for.

## The Underground Facility

Beneath the surface arrays, accessed through a sealed entrance that reads from the outside as decommissioned maintenance infrastructure, is an operations level built by the founding generation as the array management and power relay hub. It was designed to be staffed. It has not been staffed by anyone with official sanction in over two centuries.

Cassan Vale found it during his early tenure as a member of the Twelve, through the same process of careful, patient information assembly that characterises everything he does. He recognised immediately what the site's Aetheris concentration meant and what the underground facility's isolation offered. He repurposed it. No other member of the Twelve knows it exists. No record of its current use appears in any document outside Cassan's own private archive.

### What Cassan Uses It For

Research. Specifically: the study of Stage Three Aetheris progression in subjects who cannot be allowed to exist publicly — people whose advancement through the stages makes them institutionally inconvenient, whose cases produce data unavailable through the Custodian programme's more controlled conditions, and whose disappearance from the official record can be administered through the same channels the Twelve use for everything else.

The subjects are people who were disappeared during Cassan's tenure. Some are Stage Three cases identified through the CRS classification mechanism and redirected here rather than to the hull's detention facility. Some are failed Custodians — operatives whose progression toward Stage Four removed them from active service and whose continued existence posed questions the Scarlet Branch's internal records couldn't cleanly answer. Some are individuals whose specific knowledge made them worth studying rather than simply removing.

Cassan's research goal is the controlled mastery of Stage Three — finding and holding the threshold between integration and consumption, demonstrating that it can be maintained deliberately rather than progressing inevitably. The subjects are his dataset. The facility is where he tests what the Custodian programme's results suggest and the Convergence document's theoretical framework predicts.

He has not succeeded. The data is extensive. The conclusion it points toward is one he has not yet accepted.

## What Wren and Aran Find

When Wren and Aran reach the underground facility in Act Three, they find Cassan's research operation intact and occupied. The specific condition of the subjects — how many, in what states of progression, whether any are recoverable — is left open for drafting rather than locked in advance. What is locked is the register: this is the archive made physical, the suppressed history made present and human rather than documentary. Wren has spent the entire book reconstructing what the Council has done to people. This is what it looks like in a room.

Cassan's private records are here. His findings, his methodology, his three centuries of patient study compressed into his tenure's worth of applied research. Wren is the only person alive equipped to understand what they're looking at — and to understand that the data Cassan accumulated, read correctly, points unambiguously toward the conclusion he refused to accept.

---

## The Tear

The Frames site's thin boundary means tears are possible here in a way they are not within the city. They are not stable or predictable — they come and go, open and close, leave no permanent wound. Cassan has been studying their occurrence patterns and believes he has identified the conditions under which they open. He is here, in Act Three, because he believes those conditions are present.

He is not wrong about the conditions. He is wrong about what the conditions mean for him.

The tear opens. It takes him. It takes Aran, who reaches. It closes.

The Frames remain. The arrays continue their miscalibrated operation on the surface above. The underground facility is left exactly as Cassan kept it, minus Cassan, with Wren standing in the middle of it alone.

## Open Follow-Ups

- \[ \] **The specific condition of the research subjects** — how many, in what states, whether any are recoverable or communicative. Best established during drafting when the scene's specific emotional register becomes clear.
- \[ \] **The access tunnel's entry point within the hull** — where in ARBOUR|05's infrastructure the tunnel connects. Likely in the deep maintenance levels, possibly adjacent to the detention facility's own infrastructure, which would explain how the redirect of certain disappeared persons was administered without creating obvious discrepancies.
- \[ \] **What Wren does with Cassan's records** — whether they take them, destroy them, or leave them. What the records ultimately prove and to whom. Whether any of this becomes publicly known within Book One's coda or remains Wren's private knowledge going into Book Two.
- \[ \] **The Frames' visual presence in earlier chapters** — the silhouette on the horizon from the Sprawl is established canon. Worth seeding deliberately in Act One and Two scenes set in the Sprawl, so that the location carries visual weight before it becomes plot-significant.
- \[ \] **The vegetation specifically** — what grows on Cordis that would colonise a structure like this over three centuries. The plant life covering the Frames is part of the final image and worth establishing in sensory detail once Cordis's ecology is built out more fully. It should feel like the planet reclaiming something, not like neglect.
- \[ \] **The activation sequence's mechanics** — who initiates it, from where, using what from Cassan's records. The coda doesn't need this in technical detail but a scene requires it. Likely Wren, working from the underground facility or the access tunnel, using Cassan's calibration data.
- \[ \] **What the Frames produce at partial activation** — they won't reach full output immediately. What does partial activation give the Sprawl, and is that enough to matter in the coda's timeframe? Enough to be visible, enough to be felt, without resolving the scarcity problem entirely — that's a series-level arc, not a Book One resolution.
