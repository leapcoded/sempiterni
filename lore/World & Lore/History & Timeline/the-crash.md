## Overview

ARBOUR|05's destruction was not a single event. It was a cascade — each failure creating the conditions for the next, compressed into a timeline that ran from weeks to milliseconds and back out to weeks again.

It did not begin with a containment fault. It did not begin with a systems failure. It began with a man who had been changed by something the crew had no name for, in a system they had no precedent for, in the final weeks of a voyage they had no margin in.

The engineers had twenty-one seconds. They used them correctly. That is why anyone survived.

---

## Stage One — The Outer-System Stop

Before committing to final approach toward Cordis, ARBOUR|05 made a brief survey stop in KOI-8565's outer reaches — standard cautious procedure, confirming target conditions before burning the ship's remaining fuel margin on a specific descent.

During the stop, long-range scanning returned an anomaly. An artificial structure, radiating detectable energy, in a system that should have contained nothing artificial at all. The crew had no framework yet for what this meant. The concept of Penumbran civilisation — the First-Walked, the Installations, any of it — did not yet exist for them. What they had was a structure radiating usable energy in a system with a thin fuel margin, and an experienced integration team, and the pragmatic logic of a desperate voyage: take what you find, if it helps.

Dr. Jian Wei led the integration. As the crew member with the deepest working knowledge of the Kugelblitz's feed systems, he was the natural choice to assess whether harvested material and residual energy could be safely fed into the drive. He spent more sustained time in direct proximity to the Installation and what was taken from it than anyone else aboard.

What they took, they took blind — the way a person might strip copper wire from a ruin without reading a word of what's carved into the walls. Nothing about the Installation was activated, operated, or comprehended. Something was simply extracted and used. That was enough.

Wei's Aetheris affliction began here. It did not announce itself. It seeded itself in the sustained proximity of a few days' integration work and then, across the remaining weeks of final approach, quietly grew.

Several other integration team members were exposed at lower levels. Five of their medical records — scattered across a 41-year voyage log, identifiable now only in retrospect — document the spectrum of outcomes: two false leads whose symptoms faded, two slow-worsening trajectories ending in death within fifteen years of the crash, and one anchor case whose progression reached something that would now be recognised, unmistakably, as Stage Two or early Stage Three Aetheris affliction. Wei's outcome was not one of these five. His exposure was deeper, his proximity sustained, and his trajectory faster — pushing toward Stage Three in weeks rather than years, in a man surrounded by colleagues who had no language for what they were watching.

## Stage Two — The Approach

The weeks between the outer-system stop and the cascade were not dramatic. This is part of what makes them so difficult to sit with now.

What the crew registered, separately and without connecting any of it: Wei working irregular hours. Sharpened, erratic shifts in temperament — more than could be entirely explained by final-approach stress. Small errors in routine calculations, caught by others, attributed to fatigue. Nothing that, in isolation, demanded action. Nothing with a name.

Aetheris had no name yet, aboard ARBOUR|05. It had no diagnostic category, no Stage One through Four framework, no clinical precedent of any kind. What was happening to Wei had never happened to a human being anywhere before. The people around him were watching something unprecedented without knowing it was unprecedented — because to know that, they would have had to know what they were watching.

**Amara Okonkwo-Reyes** came closest. She had worked directly alongside Wei during the outer-system integration and had, at the time, noticed something she couldn't cleanly name: an inarticulable quality to his attention during the stop, as though part of him had quietly relocated somewhere else and simply not come back. She mentioned it informally to a colleague. The colleague agreed, vaguely, and had no more framework for it than she did.

Weeks later, as Wei's irregularities became harder to wave off as strain alone, Amara filed something formal. A logged concern through proper channels. A pattern she still could not fully articulate beyond: something changed during the integration stop, and it hasn't changed back.

The concern was received. Logged. Scheduled for review at the next available command-staff session.

That session was four days away. The cascade began first.

## Stage Three — The Cascade

**What Wei did** in the final moments before the cascade is established in the Kugelblitz Jettison Mathematics and is not in dispute in the historical record. He destabilised the magnetic containment field surrounding the Kugelblitz. Whether this act was directed or compelled — whether Aetheris-affliction at Stage Three constitutes direction in any meaningful sense, or whether the Convergence operates with more specificity than that — is not a question the historical record answers and is not a question anyone in Arbour currently has the framework to ask.

**What followed** is measured in fractions of a second:

At **T + 0.000s**, the burst EM wavefront reached ARBOUR|05's hull — the 50 kilotons of energy the engineers had managed to buy with their twenty-one seconds, compressed into the ship's end-on cross-section rather than its broadside. Severe. Survivable, barely.

R4 containment destabilised at **T + 0.003s**. R5 at **T + 0.007s**. The two aft reactors began a parasitic power drain on the forward three as their magnetic bottles failed, pulling R1, R2, and R3 into a cascade that the forward engineering team fought to interrupt. At **T + 0.012s**, emergency protocols attempted bottle restabilisation. At **T + 0.089s**, forward manoeuvring thrusters lost power. Navigation control went offline at **T + 0.091s**.

At approximately **T + 0.05s** — in the chaos of failing containment and the dawning, full realisation of what Wei had done and was continuing to attempt — a group of crew members moved to stop him.

**Wei was killed in the confrontation.** Survivor accounts are inconsistent. No single name can be confirmed as responsible. The historical record does not preserve who, specifically, acted, and at what point they understood there was nothing left to undo. What is established: the destabilisation Wei had triggered was already beyond any point of intervention by the time he was stopped. The cascade was running. The ship was falling.

R4 containment fully collapsed at **T + 0.340s**, partial antimatter annihilation. R5 followed at **T + 0.342s**. After that: R1/R2/R3 isolated — no longer draining, no longer supplying the aft. Emergency power to the forward sections.

At **T + 4.200s**, engineering regained partial control of R1/R2. Available thrust: approximately 18% of nominal. Assessment: insufficient to prevent descent.

## Stage Four — Twenty-One Seconds

This is worth its own section.

From the moment Wei destabilised the containment to the moment the jettison bolts fired was twenty-one seconds. The engineers knew — before the cascade, before the last moments, the engineering team that understood the drive systems knew — that the mission specification's 40.4 km safe separation distance was irrelevant to what was actually happening. That figure was calculated for a controlled jettison of a depleted, nearly-evaporated drive assembly. Not a live Kugelblitz at terminal cruise mass. Not this.

What they needed was 1,039 kilometres. What they had was a drive assembly that could be fired perpendicular at approximately 50,000 m/s relative velocity. To achieve 1,039 km of separation at that speed required 20.8 seconds.

They had twenty-one seconds.

They held containment together — fighting a destabilisation they did not yet fully understand the cause of — for the entire window. Then they fired the jettison bolts.

The burst occurred at 1,039 km of separation. The hull received 50 kilotons — 3.3 times the Hiroshima yield — against a profile representing 0.000001% of the total burst energy. The engineers had done the impossible math in twenty-one seconds and made it work.

The aft section was destroyed. R4 and R5 were gone. The ship was in uncontrolled descent. Approximately 34% of the complement was still alive.

## Stage Five — The Descent and Impact

With Wei dead and nobody else attempting to direct the ship's trajectory, ARBOUR|05's descent from that point was genuinely uncontrolled. The 18% thrust available from R1/R2 was insufficient for orbital maintenance. The ship came down where the physics of an uncontrolled, partially-thrusted ballistic re-entry took it — not at a chosen location, not at a site of any prior significance, not anywhere that anyone had identified or intended.

The site had no name. It was terrain.

At impact, the final emergency conduits severed simultaneously, collapsing what remained of R4 and R5's antimatter containment. The remaining fuel annihilated completely: 4.3 kilotons, at the impact site.

The 4.3-kiloton annihilation detonation was not simply an explosion. It occurred within a region of the Penumbran Reach where reality tears had been naturally present for millennia — stable across geological timescales, the slow product of KOI-8565's binary stellar dynamics and the Convergence's pre-existing pressure against this reality's boundary. The energy release permanently widened several of the tears nearest the impact zone. Aetheris intensity in the region increased by approximately 340% in the immediate aftermath.

ARBOUR|05's wreckage fused with the affected terrain across the impact zone — not embedded at a single point but scattered across the widened tears, intertwined with the altered Aetheris field at a material level. There was no single crash site. There was a debris field. There is only ever the debris field.

Arbour was built where it crashed, not the other way around.

## The Inscription

At some point between the jettison and the impact — most likely in the chaos immediately following Wei's death, while the surviving crew managed what could still be managed — someone went to the jettison control station and wrote on the bulkhead above it.

Three words and a number:

*"40.4. We knew. Sorry."*

Written in a hand that matched Chief Engineer **[NAME — TBD through drafting]**'s personnel file.

What "we knew" refers to is deliberately ambiguous and should remain so. It may mean the engineers knew the 40.4 km mission-spec figure was inadequate for a live Kugelblitz jettison. It may mean they knew something was wrong with Wei, before the end, and could not or did not act on it in time. Both readings are available. The person who wrote it did not separate them, or could not, or chose not to.

The chief engineer died in the impact event.

The inscription was found by a maintenance crew in the second generation. It was reported to the proto-Council. The report was received, noted, and filed under reference **AZ-1-0003-I** in the earliest Tabularium records. It does not appear in any index. It can only be found if you already know the reference number.

The reference number AZ-1-0003-I appears in one place: a footnote in a founding generation engineering report, misfiled in the biological sciences archive rather than the technical archive, where it has sat for centuries in the wrong section, uncatalogued, waiting.

## Stage Six — The First Hours

Thirty-four percent of ARBOUR|05's complement survived the impact. The figure compounds everything: the EM cascade, the structural destruction of the aft section, the antimatter annihilation events mid-flight, the impact itself. The people who survived were, by and large, the people who happened to be in the forward sections when the burst hit — command crew, research staff, colonisation logistics teams, a handful of engineering staff who'd been called forward during final approach for reasons unrelated to what then happened.

What the first hours looked like:

The ship was structurally compromised and geographically unfamiliar. The surviving reactors — R1 and R2, partially drained, R3 limping — provided power to the forward sections only. Navigation and SEED's navigation partition had lost data during the cascade; ARC (then SEED) had begun the memory loss that would accumulate across three centuries into the degradation visible in Arbour's present day, though nobody in those first hours understood that was what was happening.

The colonisation infrastructure in the forward sections was intact. The agricultural systems, seed banks, atmospheric processing equipment, water recycling — the things the ship was designed to deliver — were available. The things ARBOUR|05 had been designed to do first, before anything else was attempted, could still be done.

What the founding generation did with those first hours: triage. Account for survivors. Stabilise the forward reactors. Establish a perimeter. Begin the work of surviving. The grief — for colleagues, for the voyage's purpose, for everything the crash had meant — was real and was also, by necessity, not the first priority.

The Aetheris spike registered immediately in the survivors with any susceptibility. There was no language for what they were experiencing. It was folded into shock.

The solar collection arrays — designed to be erected first, before any permanent settlement was built, to provide the energy supplement the founding generation always knew the reactors would need — were erected in the first weeks on open terrain west of the debris field. They were never calibrated or activated. The forward reactors held. The founding generation prioritised the immediate over the strategic, and the solar arrays became the Frames: the infrastructure of a plan they survived past needing, standing on open terrain over a subsurface Installation nobody had yet mapped.

## What the Crash Was Not

**Not a straightforward accident.** The official account — Wei's affliction, unrecognised, a tragedy — is true in every verifiable particular and incomplete in the way the Convergence's involvement makes everything incomplete. Wei was used. The crash was directed. Neither the Council's version nor The Margin's sabotage theory reaches this truth; both are less terrible than it.

**Not sabotage.** The Margin's foundational document builds its case on a genuine account of Wei's behaviour — purposeful, concealed, directed — and draws the only conclusion that behaviour seems to support without the concept of Aetheris-compulsion to explain it. The sabotage theory is wrong. It is wrong in a way that took real, honest work by a real historian to arrive at, from evidence that genuinely looks the way it looks.

**Not the end of Wei.** What Wei was doing in those final weeks was not fully Wei. Whether there is a moment in the sequence where he was aware of what was happening to him — where the compulsion receded briefly enough for something like horror to register — is not established in the historical record. It is one of the things the record cannot see into, and one of the questions with real weight for how this beats against a reader.
