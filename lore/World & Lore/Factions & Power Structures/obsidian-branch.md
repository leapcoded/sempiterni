## What It Is

The Obsidian Branch is Arbour's enforcement institution. It initiates postings, administers the detention facility in the bowels of the hull, maintains public order across the city's tiers, and operates the surveillance infrastructure jointly with Azure Branch. It is the Branch that most Arbour residents interact with when the system turns its attention toward them, and the Branch most residents try hardest never to interact with at all.

Its official mandate is the safety and security of Arbour's population. That mandate is real — most Obsidian Branch members believe in it sincerely, and the Branch's day-to-day work does include genuine public safety functions. The mandate is also the framework through which the Branch has spent three centuries doing things that have nothing to do with safety, and everything to do with the maintenance of the existing order. The two are not separable from the inside. From the outside, the pattern is visible in who the Branch's attention lands on, how heavily, and how often.

## Internal Structure

### Public Order Division

The uniformed face of Obsidian Branch. Public Order officers are the Branch members most Arbour residents can identify on sight — their uniform marks them before they speak, which is the point. Visible presence is part of the function. A Public Order officer standing at a district junction in the Sprawl is doing enforcement work simply by standing there.

Public Order handles street-level operations: patrols, crowd management, first-response enforcement, the physical presence at community hubs where postings are displayed. It is the most junior entry point into the Branch — new recruits typically begin here regardless of which division they are ultimately tracking toward — and the most numerically large. Most people who enter Public Order stay in Public Order.

The division operates differently by tier. In Luminary and upper Meridian, Public Order has a lighter visible presence — more ceremonial, more deferential in how it engages with residents, less likely to exercise discretionary initiative. In the Sprawl, it operates with broader latitude and less accountability to the communities it polices. The formal rules are the same. The application is not.

### Investigations Division

Plain clothes. Case-building. The people who decide whether an incident becomes a formal posting.

This is where Obsidian Branch's real discretionary power lives. An Investigations officer who opens a case against a resident has started a process that is difficult to stop once it begins — the investigation produces a record, the record supports a filing, the filing produces a posting. Equally, an Investigations officer who declines to open a case has made that incident disappear as far as the formal system is concerned. The decision to open or not open is not reviewed externally in any meaningful way. It is the Branch's internal judgment.

Investigations officers are selected through the Branch's most rigorous examination track — patience, pattern recognition, comfort with moral ambiguity, the ability to hold a case together across months of incremental work. They are also selected, less formally, for the ability to operate in the grey space between what the law says and what the Branch needs. Not every Investigations officer knows they've been selected for this. They believe they've been selected for competence, which they have. The two qualities turn out to be useful together.

### Joint Surveillance Unit

The surveillance infrastructure that runs beneath Arbour's visible life — monitoring movement patterns, flagging communication anomalies, tracking data pad usage, maintaining the network of human intelligence that no purely technical system can replace — is administered by a combined team drawing from both Obsidian Branch and Azure Branch, operating under joint leadership with equal representation from each.

The joint leadership structure means neither Branch fully controls what the unit knows. Intelligence flows to both Branch heads simultaneously. In practice this creates its own politics: what the unit shares, when, and with whom is a constant negotiation between two institutions with overlapping but not identical interests. Azure Branch wants to know about threats to infrastructure. Obsidian Branch wants to know about threats to order. These categories overlap often enough to sustain the partnership and diverge often enough to create friction.

The unit has no uniformed presence. Its members are never publicly identified as surveillance operatives. Their official Branch affiliation, if it appears in a record at all, is listed against their originating Branch rather than the unit itself.

### Custody Division

Administers the detention facility in the lowest accessible levels of ARBOUR|05's hull. Manages intake, conditions, duration tracking, and release processing. Coordinates with Investigations on transfer of detained residents and with the debt resolution authority on cases that arrive through the financial system rather than through a criminal filing.

Custody is the division with the least public visibility and the most direct daily contact with what the Branch's power actually looks like in practice. Officers who work Custody long enough develop a specific relationship to the institution that other division members don't share — they've seen what comes in and what comes out, and they've seen what doesn't come out, and the Branch's framework for making sense of that shapes them in particular ways.

### The Fifth Division

No public-facing name. Obsidian Branch does not confirm its existence in any official documentation. Its function is the quiet removal of residents who have become inconvenient to the institution or to the directives the Branch receives from above — containment, redirection, suppression of knowledge, and in some cases the management of outcomes that require no further record.

It is very small. Fewer than a dozen active members at any given time. It operates entirely outside the standard case-filing system — no intake records, no posted outcomes, no registry annotations beyond the deliberate bureaucratic silences it leaves in other people's files. Its members are identified through a secondary layer of selection that occurs after formal induction, through a process only the Branch head and one or two senior Investigations figures are aware of. Not every Obsidian Branch member knows it exists, and those who do know better than to speak about it in contexts that might be recorded.

**What it does:** The Fifth Division's primary method is administrative rather than physical. It produces paperwork. A resident who needs to stop being a problem becomes, through the Fifth Division's work, a person who voluntarily resigned their position, who transferred to a different district, who chose to leave Arbour entirely, whose ARC record reflects a life that continued normally in a direction nobody will look. The quiet removal mechanism it administers is precisely this — the erasure of people through official channels rather than through violence. Wren Emberlain's record showing a career transition rather than what actually happened is a Fifth Division product. The registry entries annotated *suspended without further notation* are its most visible trace.

**What it does not do:** The Fifth Division does not deploy physically against targets. It is not equipped for it, not selected for it, and not authorised for it. When a situation requires physical presence, force, or field operations beyond what Custody can manage, that falls to other institutions operating under their own mandates. The Fifth Division does not coordinate with those institutions and is not aware of their specific operations. The separation is deliberate.

**The Margin's knowledge of it:** The Margin knows the Fifth Division exists in the same way it knows most things — through accumulated pattern recognition, through the testimony of people who survived adjacent to its operations, through the gaps in records that are too consistent to be accidental. It has no confirmed member names. It has never successfully infiltrated it. What The Margin holds is a shape and a suspicion, not a file.

## The Examination

Obsidian Branch runs a two-track examination process: a standard track for candidates entering Public Order, and an extended track for candidates flagged for Investigations or above.

The standard track tests physical aptitude, procedural knowledge, and the ability to exercise discretionary judgment within established frameworks. It is designed to produce officers who are capable, rule-following, and comfortable with the authority the uniform conveys.

The extended track adds a psychological and aptitude dimension that is not publicly detailed. Candidates on this track undergo assessment sessions that look, from the outside, like structured interviews, but are designed to evaluate how a candidate responds to moral complexity, how they relate to the exercise of power, and how effectively they operate when the formal rules are ambiguous or silent. The Branch is selecting for people who can hold a case together when the evidence is incomplete, pursue a target through means that don't appear in any manual, and maintain their institutional loyalty when the work they're doing is the kind that doesn't get documented.

Most candidates who pass the extended track believe they have been selected for their competence and judgment, which is accurate. The examination also selected for something else, and most of them never have cause to notice.

## Internal Culture

The Obsidian Branch runs on a genuine institutional belief in safety and order. This is not cynical — most members, including most senior members, sincerely hold that what they do is necessary, that Arbour would be worse without them, that the populations they police are safer for their presence. The Branch's induction ceremony, its oath, its internal language of service and protection — these are not performances. They are the framework through which members understand their own work.

The Branch selects, at every level, for people who respond well to this framework. The psychological examination identifies candidates predisposed to the safety-and-order worldview. The probationary period embeds it. The divisional culture sustains it. By the time a member is senior enough to see clearly what the Branch does at its less visible levels, they have spent years inside a framework that makes those things legible as necessary rather than excessive.

The parallel to military or national service is close and deliberate — Obsidian members think of themselves as people who do difficult work so others don't have to. They carry the weight of enforcement so Arbour can function. The work is sometimes ugly and the world would be naïve to pretend otherwise, but the alternative is disorder, and disorder is worse. This is what the Branch believes about itself.

What the Branch actually does — consistently, across three centuries, in patterns visible only to people who are looking at the aggregate rather than the individual case — is exercise power over residents who have the least means to resist it, using safety as the framework that makes each individual instance of that power legible as protection. The Sprawl is policed more heavily than Luminary not because it generates more genuine threat to public safety, but because it generates less resistance to the exercise of that power, and because the people making the decisions about where to direct Branch resources are not Sprawl residents.

Most Obsidian Branch members would sincerely and vigorously reject this characterisation. That sincerity is the most important thing about it.

## The Twelve

One or two figures within Obsidian Branch's senior leadership know the Twelve exist. They do not know all twelve members. They know that certain directives — arriving through ordinary Outer Council channels, formatted as routine policy, but carrying an unusual specificity and force — originate somewhere above the Outer Council's actual decision-making capacity. They have learned not to ask where.

What they receive in practice is operational direction without visible origin: a particular case that should be moved from Investigations to the fifth division, a particular individual whose ARC record should reflect a career transition rather than what actually happened, a particular threshold of tolerance for a particular kind of knowledge that should be treated as a serious infraction. The direction is clear. The source is not their concern.

Below this level, Obsidian Branch operates without knowledge of the Twelve. Branch members execute policy they believe comes from the Outer Council. The framework is consistent enough, and their own institutional worldview coherent enough, that the directives never seem to require an explanation that isn't already available inside the Branch's own logic.
