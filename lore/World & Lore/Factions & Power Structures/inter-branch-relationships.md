# Inter-Branch Relationships

*Lives in: World & Lore → Factions & Power Structures. Companion to Arbour Branches Overview (shared framework and brooch system) and the individual Branch documents. This document covers the full web of inter-Branch dynamics — bilateral relationships, triangular tensions, and how the whole structure looks from above. Individual Branch articles cover internal culture and specific disputes in more depth; this document is for orientation and cross-reference.*

---

## The Shape of the Web

Seven Branches. Not a hierarchy in any formal sense — each Branch head reports to the Outer Council, not to each other, and no Branch is officially subordinate to another. In practice the web has gradient and weight: some Branches are more powerful, some more dependent, some more resented, and the informal hierarchy that results from three centuries of accumulated leverage, mutual obligation, and carefully maintained advantage is more real than any org chart the Council has ever produced.

The web has two poles. At one end: Azure and Obsidian, the alliance that has functioned continuously since the founding generation and that administers more of Arbour's daily life than any other pairing. At the other: Ivory, hollowed out, dependent on every other Branch for resources it can no longer reliably produce in exchange, sustained by institutional inertia rather than operational relevance. Between them: Verdant and Golden locked in a structural conflict neither can resolve, Scarlet insulated by classified independence that everyone else resents, and Violet underfunded and narratively sophisticated about exactly why.

---

## The Primary Alliance — Azure and Obsidian

The closest pairing in the web, and the oldest. It predates the Branch formalisation — the people who maintained the ship's technical systems and the people who maintained order aboard it had overlapping interests from the first week of the voyage, and three centuries on Cordis have only deepened the entanglement.

The operational expression of this alliance is the Joint Surveillance Unit, which draws resources and personnel from both Branches and reports simultaneously to both Branch heads. The JSU is, by design, something neither Branch fully controls — intelligence flows to both simultaneously, and what the unit does with what it finds is a constant negotiation between two institutions whose interests overlap often enough to sustain the partnership and diverge often enough to create friction. Azure wants to know about threats to infrastructure. Obsidian wants to know about threats to order. These are frequently the same threat, and occasionally they are not, and the occasions when they are not are where the alliance shows its seams.

The cultural gap between the two Branches is real and persistent. Azure's ritual-engineering culture produces people who are precise, procedural, and deeply invested in the authority of established method. Obsidian's enforcement culture produces people who are pragmatic, outcome-focused, and more comfortable with discretion than with procedure. They work well together because they need to. They do not particularly like each other, as institutions, in a way that has calcified across generations into a specific professional register: correct, cooperative, and carrying three hundred years of accumulated awareness that the other Branch would make different choices if it were in charge.

**The specific fault line:** what to do when ARC produces anomalous outputs that suggest infrastructure failure. Azure's instinct is to suppress, reclassify, and manage the information internally — the Deviation Log approach. Obsidian's instinct, on the rare occasions it becomes aware of such outputs, is to treat them as potential security information. The JSU has processed several Deviation Log anomalies that Obsidian thought had security implications and Azure insisted were technical matters. The outcomes of those cases are not in any document that both Branch heads have access to.

## The Primary Conflict — Verdant and Golden

The most structurally embedded tension in the web, and the one with the least prospect of resolution.

Verdant controls what exists. Golden controls who gets it. These two functions have fundamentally different relationships to the same resources, and each Branch has leverage the other cannot do without. Verdant cannot distribute what it produces without Golden's infrastructure. Golden cannot distribute what it doesn't have without Verdant's production. The dependency is mutual and the resentment it generates is therefore also mutual, flowing in both directions simultaneously with different textures.

Verdant's resentment is upstream: the Branch that does the essential biological work of keeping Arbour fed has less institutional power than the Branch that hands the food out, and this has been true for long enough that it has become a matter of identity. Verdant members tend toward quiet, persistent grievance about a power imbalance they cannot change and will not stop mentioning.

Golden's resentment is different: the Branch that has the clearest view of Arbour's actual resource position — the gap between what the official supply figures say and what actually exists — is forced to manage that knowledge through a network of deniable arrangements and shadow supply infrastructure that Verdant pretends not to know about and that Golden cannot officially acknowledge. Golden is doing necessary, unglamorous work to keep Arbour functioning at a level Verdant's official production cannot sustain alone, and receiving neither credit nor clean institutional cover for it.

The conflict has its own choreography by now. Predictable positions, predictable escalations, predictable resolutions that leave the structural tension exactly where it was. Both Branch heads have had versions of the same argument multiple times, with different specifics, producing the same outcome. Neither expects this to change. What they negotiate is not resolution but management — keeping the conflict at a level that doesn't require Outer Council intervention, because Outer Council intervention would mean scrutiny neither Branch wants applied to certain numbers.

**The condenser subtext:** Azure has filed three reports over the past century recommending a full audit and recalibration of Arbour's solar condenser systems, which fall in jurisdictional territory that Verdant claims. Verdant has blocked all three on jurisdictional grounds. The actual reason involves questions about who is responsible for a miscalibration that has been running for decades and what the real nutritional throughput numbers look like if the condensers were operating correctly. Neither Branch wants those questions asked. The condensers continue to run at miscalibrated settings. This is filed, in the JSU's broader surveillance picture, as a known inter-Branch dispute and not investigated further.

## The Independence Problem — Scarlet

Scarlet generates resentment across all other Branches without exception. This is not a bilateral relationship. It is a structural condition.

A Branch that does not account for itself to peer institutions, that receives classified operational direction from a source other Branches cannot scrutinise, that has military deployment authority that can theoretically override normal jurisdictional limits, and that processes inter-Branch complaints with consistent courteous non-engagement is an irritant regardless of whether it exercises any of this authority in a given week. The other Branches resent Scarlet the way institutions resent any peer that has been exempted from the accountability they themselves must perform. It would be resentful even if Scarlet never did anything. Scarlet does things.

**Scarlet and Obsidian** share the most specific tension. Both have security and enforcement mandates. Both operate within Arbour's territory and occasionally in its surrounding region. The jurisdictional boundary between them is formally defined and practically contested: Obsidian handles internal order, Scarlet handles Aetheris-adjacent threats and external deployment. The boundary of "Aetheris-adjacent" is defined by Scarlet. Obsidian has opinions about this definition that it cannot express in any forum where Scarlet would have to respond to them.

The JSU is, among other things, a mechanism by which Azure and Obsidian maintain some visibility into Scarlet-relevant territory — the surveillance infrastructure that both Branches contribute to produces information about the Badlands, about candidate-profile individuals, about Custodian deployment patterns, that Scarlet cannot fully suppress without withdrawing from the JSU, which it will not do because the JSU provides Scarlet with information it values. The result is a three-way information negotiation in which each Branch gets partial visibility into the others' activities and all three are managing what they share carefully.

**Scarlet and Golden** have a quieter relationship than either has with others, grounded in mutual pragmatism. Golden's shadow supply network and Scarlet's Badlands operations overlap in territory and in the use of Sprawl-tier contractors who don't ask questions. Neither Branch officially acknowledges the other's presence in that territory. Both are aware that the other is there. The informal arrangement has held for two generations: Golden doesn't ask what Scarlet's contractors are doing in the same Badlands regions where Golden's extraction operations run, and Scarlet doesn't examine the supply chains through which certain materials reach Arbour without appearing in any official import record.

## The Marginal Positions — Violet and Ivory

**Violet** is the youngest Branch, the most recently formalised, and the most chronically underfunded relative to its mandate. Its budget has been lower than its stated requirements since its founding and shows no sign of approaching adequacy, because every other Branch can make a more direct case for resources in terms the Outer Council treats as essential. Cultural authorisation and performance licensing are not infrastructure maintenance or food production or military deployment. They are, visibly, easier to cut.

The resentment this produces is different in character from any other inter-Branch tension in the web. Violet members understand narrative — they are, institutionally, the people whose professional formation involves thinking about story, meaning, and how information is received. The Branch's chronic underfunding has, across generations, produced people who are expert at framing their situation in terms that generate sympathy and who know exactly which other Branches are responsible for their position and why those Branches made those choices. Violet is the Branch that could, if it chose, build the most compelling public case against any of its peers. It does not do this. The implicit threat of this capability is more useful than its exercise.

Violet's specific inter-Branch tensions: with Obsidian, over the enforcement of unauthorised performance — Obsidian enforces what Violet is supposed to license, which means Obsidian has operational stake in territory Violet considers its mandate, and the two Branches have different instincts about how firmly the line should be held. With the education body, over cultural curriculum — whether Violet has formal input into what cultural content the education system transmits is contested, and the contest is partly jurisdictional and partly ideological. With Golden, over the economics of authorised performance — Golden's distribution infrastructure is involved in the commercialisation of cultural works, which requires Violet authorisation but produces Cyph flows that Golden has opinions about.

**Ivory** is the most isolated position in the web. The medical Branch has a formal mandate, formal standing, and no meaningful leverage over any other institution because the pipeline that would generate that leverage — trained practitioners providing essential services that other Branches cannot replicate — has collapsed across generations. Ivory cannot threaten to withhold what it no longer reliably produces.

What Ivory has instead is dependency in all directions, which every other Branch is aware of and manages in ways that serve their own interests. Azure's Continuance Corps maintains the reactor power that runs Ivory's medical equipment — a dependency Azure has never had to exercise as leverage because it has never needed to. Golden's distribution network handles medical supply chains, including the pharmaceutical compounds that the Sprawl's self-taught healers cannot synthesise — a dependency Golden has made structural by ensuring that the supply relationships run through Golden's infrastructure rather than Ivory's. Obsidian has jurisdictional access to the detention facility that houses some of the people who receive Ivory's most minimal provision — a dependency that requires Ivory to cooperate with Obsidian's management of that facility without the standing to ask questions about what happens there.

Ivory knows, at the institutional level, that it has become an administrative shell sustained by the other Branches' need for a medical function to formally exist rather than by the actual delivery of that function. This knowledge is not openly discussed. It shapes everything.

## Triangular Dynamics

Some of the web's most significant tensions run through three-Branch configurations rather than bilateral pairs.

**Azure / Obsidian / Scarlet** — the surveillance triangle. The JSU gives Azure and Obsidian shared visibility. Scarlet receives JSU outputs without contributing equivalently to the shared pool. Azure and Obsidian have, separately and without coordination, developed working assumptions about what Scarlet is doing in the regions the JSU covers. Neither has shared those assumptions with the other, because doing so would require acknowledging surveillance activity that exceeds what either Branch is supposed to be running independently. Scarlet is aware of both sets of assumptions and has taken steps to manage what the JSU's coverage actually reaches.

**Verdant / Golden / Ivory** — the supply triangle. Verdant produces. Golden distributes. Ivory requires. The three-way dependency means that any disruption to the Verdant/Golden conflict immediately affects Ivory's capacity to function, which neither Verdant nor Golden wants to be seen as causing. Ivory's weakness is, paradoxically, a minor stabilising factor in the Verdant/Golden conflict: neither Branch can push the other to actual breakdown without the resulting Ivory crisis becoming a Council-level problem that neither wants.

**Violet / Obsidian / Golden** — the cultural economy triangle. Violet licenses performance. Obsidian enforces against unlicensed performance. Golden handles the distribution of commercially reproduced cultural works. The three Branches have different interests in where the line between licensed and unlicensed sits, different interests in how strictly that line is enforced, and different interests in how the Cyph flows generated by cultural commerce are categorised. None of them has ever sat down to negotiate these interests jointly, because doing so would require each Branch to reveal how much it is managing the others' blind spots.

## The View From Above

The Twelve observe the inter-Branch web with the advantage of visibility no single Branch head possesses: they can see across all the bilateral tensions simultaneously, and they have spent decades ensuring that the tensions remain managed rather than resolved.

A web of inter-Branch rivalry is, from the Twelve's perspective, a feature rather than a problem. Branches that are focused on each other's territory and resources are not focused on the structure above them. The Verdant/Golden conflict has, for two generations, absorbed the attention of two Branch heads who might otherwise have developed the kind of institutional coherence that asks awkward questions about resource flows at the Twelve level. The Azure/Obsidian alliance is useful and contained — useful because the JSU produces intelligence the Twelve value, contained because both Branches' cultures make them fundamentally conservative and unlikely to threaten anything.

**Cassan** moves through the web with specific, deliberate purpose. He is not aligned with any Branch head and not in competition with any Branch as an institution. What he is doing is using the web's existing tensions as operational cover: the Verdant/Golden conflict provides justification for Golden's shadow supply network, which provides cover for some of the Frames facility's supply chain. The Azure/Obsidian alliance's surveillance infrastructure generates candidate-profile intelligence that reaches Cassan through channels Voss does not know exist. Violet's chronic underfunding creates a class of skilled, resentful people who understand narrative and whose services are available for arrangements that don't appear in official Branch records.

He does not control the web. He reads it more accurately than anyone else does, and he acts where the readings tell him the gap is.
