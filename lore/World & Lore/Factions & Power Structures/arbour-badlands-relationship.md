# Arbour and the Badlands

*Lives in: World & Lore → Factions & Power Structures. Companion to Arbour City Geography (the hull-to-city cross-section and Arbour's territorial boundary), Scarlet Branch (Custodian deployment outside Arbour), The Frames (Installation-adjacent operations), and the Wayfarer social structure documents (communities operating in contested territory).*

---

## The Official Position

Arbour's official position on the Badlands is that there is no official position. The territory beyond the city's boundary is described, when described at all, as dangerous and largely uncharted — Aetheris-heavy terrain, unstable in ways that make sustained habitation inadvisable, populated by the kind of people who either couldn't make it in Arbour or chose not to try. The Council does not claim jurisdiction. It does not formally disclaim jurisdiction either. The Badlands exist in a carefully maintained institutional silence: not Arbour's territory, not Arbour's problem, not something that requires a policy because policy implies acknowledgement and acknowledgement implies responsibility.

This position is convenient. It is also, on the available evidence, a lie of omission that has been sustained for at least two generations.

## What Arbour Actually Does There

### Resource Extraction

The Badlands produce materials the city needs and cannot produce internally. Specific mineral deposits, raw materials for certain manufacturing processes, biological matter from Cordis's open ecology that the city's closed-loop systems cannot replicate. The extraction is not officially acknowledged and does not appear in the public-facing supply chain documentation. It appears instead in Golden Branch's shadow supply network — the same infrastructure that handles external trade through deniable intermediaries — as unspecified external sourcing from unnamed contractors.

In practice, the contractors are Sprawl-tier residents with enough Badlands experience to operate the extraction sites and not enough institutional standing to ask questions about the arrangement. Custodians provide security when needed. The work is unglamorous, genuinely dangerous, and paid at a rate that is enough to make it attractive to people with few other options. Nobody involved calls it what it is.

### Installation Monitoring and Control

The Badlands contains active Penumbran Installation sites — relics of the First-Walked civilisation, still generating or channelling Aetheris in ways that the founding generation documented poorly and subsequent generations have understood only through the Custodian programme's accumulated operational data.

Scarlet Branch has quietly claimed specific active sites. Not through any formal territorial declaration — there is no document, no map, no policy instrument that establishes Scarlet's access to particular Badlands locations. There are operational protocols, passed down through the Custodian programme, that designate certain sites as programme assets: locations used for controlled induction exposure, locations monitored for Aetheris concentration and activity shifts, locations that Custodians are deployed to maintain and protect without the deployment ever being described in those terms.

Voss Shearwall knows these sites exist and knows Scarlet has operational interest in them. She frames this, accurately, as a function of the induction programme: the controlled exposure process requires specific environments, and the Badlands Installation sites provide them. What she does not know — or has not examined — is the degree to which the monitoring of these sites also feeds intelligence upward to the Twelve, and specifically to Cassan Vale, about Aetheris concentration shifts that matter for his research at the Frames facility.

The sites are not public knowledge. Wayfarers and Badlands communities know Installation sites exist and generally avoid the most active ones, having learned through generations of proximity that close contact with certain structures produces specific, unwanted effects in some individuals. This avoidance knowledge is oral, practical, and imprecise — they know to keep distance from particular landmarks; they do not know why Custodians appear periodically to inspect them.

### Candidate Sourcing

Aetheris exposure does not affect everyone. This is established fact within the Custodian programme and observable in Badlands communities: people can live in proximity to active Installation sites for generations without developing anything beyond background sensitivity. The Gloaming — Stage Two presentation — is not an inevitable consequence of sustained Aetheris proximity. It is a response that occurs in some individuals, for reasons the programme does not fully understand, and does not occur in others exposed to identical conditions.

A small number of people living in high-Aetheris Badlands territory develop genuine Stage Two or Stage Three presentation without any institutional framework for what is happening to them. They do not have the Custodian programme's language. They do not have suppressant access. What they have is a set of experiences — sensory, perceptual, increasingly difficult to manage — that the communities around them may interpret as illness, gift, instability, or spiritual significance depending on local tradition and the individual's own presentation.

Scarlet Branch's monitoring of Badlands communities identifies these individuals. The surveillance is patient and ongoing: it is not a sweep but an accumulation of observation across years, watching for the specific pattern — the sensory shift, the withdrawal, the quality of attention that marks someone whose perception has extended beyond ordinary biological range — that distinguishes genuine Stage Two from ordinary Badlands eccentricity.

What happens when an individual is identified depends on the assessment.

Some are approached for the Custodian programme through what presents as standard Scarlet recruitment: a contact, an offer, a carefully incomplete disclosure. The programme prefers candidates with existing Badlands exposure because the baseline acclimation reduces the shock of controlled induction and because the practical skills — navigation, field survival, the specific competence of someone who has spent years reading difficult terrain — are operationally valuable. These candidates enter the programme legitimately. Voss knows about them. She considers Badlands recruitment a useful talent pipeline.

Some are not suitable for the programme — too far progressed, presenting in ways that suggest rapid Stage Four trajectory, or lacking the psychological profile the programme requires. These individuals are not left in place. They are redirected through a different channel. Voss does not know about this channel. She knows Scarlet monitors Badlands communities. She believes the monitoring's only output is the legitimate recruitment pipeline. She has not asked what happens to the individuals who are identified but not recruited, because the programme has taught her, among other things, when not to look too closely at a source.

The different channel is Cassan's. The underground facility beneath the Frames receives, among its subjects, individuals sourced from Badlands communities — people whose Stage Three presentation was identified through Scarlet's monitoring apparatus, redirected through a transfer mechanism that sits above Voss's visibility, and delivered to a research context she does not know exists. They arrive without records. They do not return.

## What Badlands Communities Know

The communities operating in the Badlands — shadow settlements, Wayfarer caravans in transit, independent nomadic groups — have an imprecise but functional understanding of the territory's dangers. They know:

That certain landmarks are to be avoided. That proximity to some structures produces specific effects in some people, and that this does not happen to everyone, and that there is no reliable way to know in advance whether you are the kind of person it happens to. That occasional outsiders pass through the territory on unclear business and it is better not to engage with them. That some people from their communities leave — recruited, they are told, for city work — and are not heard from again, which is not unusual enough to be remarkable since many people who go to the city do not write home.

They do not know about the installation monitoring. They do not know about the candidate sourcing operation. They do not know that the occasional outsiders represent Scarlet Branch. The practical knowledge they have accumulated through generations of living in this territory is accurate, useful, and insufficient to protect them from a surveillance operation they have no framework to identify.

## Aran Sunderwood in This Context

Aran has been ranging the Badlands for over two decades. He has the practical avoidance knowledge his caravan accumulated and passed on — he knows which kinds of terrain produce what he has always called the Hum, knows to read the quality of that sensation as a navigational signal without ever having had framework for what it is. His long exposure to moderate Aetheris concentration across his ranging career is part of what made him a viable vector for the click: not because Badlands exposure guaranteed Stage Three, but because it primed the relevant biological systems over decades in a way that made him susceptible when the Convergence produced the conditions.

He has not been identified by Scarlet's monitoring. His presentation — the click as recent phenomenon, the slow accumulation of the Hum as navigational tool — does not match the visible Stage Two profile the monitoring looks for. He moves through the territory they watch without being seen by it, which is consistent with who he is: a generalist built for exactly this kind of passage, read by the terrain without being fully legible to the people trying to read the terrain.
