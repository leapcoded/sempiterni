# The Scarlet Branch

*Lives in: World & Lore → Factions & Power Structures. Companion to Political Systems (Branch structure, the Twelve), World Systems (Aetheris — four stages of exposure), and the Obsidian Branch (enforcement infrastructure, inter-Branch relationships). The Custodian enhancement specifics and deterioration outcomes are flagged as open — see Open Follow-Ups.*

---

## What It Is

The Scarlet Branch administers the Custodian programme. Beyond that, its mandate is not publicly detailed. Other Branches know Scarlet exists, know it has military deployment authority, and know that its internal operations are classified in ways that make their own classified work look transparent. The resentment this generates is real and consistent across all other Branches. Scarlet does not appear to notice.

Its independence from the normal inter-Branch accountability structure is not an accident or an oversight. It was designed in, by people who understood that what the programme does cannot survive scrutiny from institutions that don't know what Aetheris is. The Scarlet Branch head receives directives that appear to originate from the Outer Council, formatted as routine policy with unusual specificity and force. The current Branch head has learned not to examine the origin too carefully. This has served the programme well.

## The Custodian Programme

### What Custodians Are

Custodians are Arbour's special forces — small in number, extraordinarily capable, deployable inside and outside the city in ways that no other Branch's personnel are. They operate where ordinary enforcement cannot reach or cannot be seen to reach: beyond Arbour's territorial boundary, in contexts that require deniability, against threats that the Council cannot acknowledge publicly.

They are also something the other Branches do not know about and would not have a framework to understand if they did: people in whom Aetheris exposure has been deliberately induced, monitored, and where possible stabilised at a level that produces operational enhancement without immediate deterioration.

### Selection

Selection for the Custodian programme runs entirely outside the standard Branch examination and induction pipeline. Scarlet Branch does not recruit through the education body's qualification tracks or through the sponsorship networks that feed other Branches. It watches.

Candidates are identified through observation — physical capability, tactical instinct, psychological profile, the specific combination of qualities that makes someone both highly effective and capable of operating in conditions of extreme isolation, moral complexity, and classified constraint. Some candidates are drawn from existing Branch members, identified as exceptional within their own institutions and quietly approached. Others are found outside the Branch structure entirely — Sprawl residents with the right profile, Wayfarer-adjacent individuals whose ranging experience gives them capabilities that city-born candidates lack, people the standard system would never have selected because the standard system wasn't looking for what they have.

Being approached is not an offer that comes with full information. Candidates are told enough to consent and not enough to fully understand what they're consenting to. The programme has found that full disclosure at the selection stage produces a different candidate pool than partial disclosure, and the programme has decided it prefers its current pool.

### Induction and Aetheris Exposure

Once selected, candidates enter a programme that runs in two parallel tracks: operational training and controlled Aetheris exposure.

The operational training is what it appears to be — the most demanding and comprehensive preparation for combat, fieldwork, and special operations that Arbour's institutions can produce. Custodians who never progress beyond Stage One exposure are still extraordinarily capable operatives by any standard the city can apply.

The Aetheris exposure is deliberate and graduated. Candidates are brought into proximity with active Installation sites or Aetheris-concentrated environments under controlled conditions, at measured intervals, with the programme monitoring their biological response. The goal is to find and hold the threshold between Stage Two and Stage Three — the point at which perception extends beyond normal biological range, the Convergence becomes perceptible as a physical presence, and the candidate gains operational capabilities that cannot be trained through any other means.

What this feels like from the inside mirrors what any individual experiences at Stage Two and Three — the shimmer entering peripheral vision, then the deeper stage: something felt in the body rather than heard with the ears, irregular, like a signal from a source that cannot be located by ordinary means. For Custodians, this is interpreted through the programme's framework: a tactical asset, a perceptual edge, something to be developed and directed rather than questioned. The programme gives its members a language for the experience that keeps it operational rather than existential. This language is, in its way, as much a framework as any theology. It serves the same function: making the inexplicable legible enough to live with.

### The Spectrum of Response

Aetheris affects individuals differently, and the programme has known this since its earliest operations. Some candidates stabilise at Stage Two or the early threshold of Stage Three and remain there indefinitely — their enhancement becomes a permanent operational baseline, their deterioration either absent or progressing so slowly as to be functionally invisible across a career. These are the programme's ideal outcomes, and they are not predictable in advance.

Others progress. Slowly in some cases — a candidate who holds Stage Three for years before the progression accelerates, remaining operational through most of their service before the timeline shortens. Rapidly in others — a candidate whose biology moves through Stage Three toward Stage Four within months of induction, fast enough that the programme's monitoring catches it and the operative's deployment is adjusted accordingly, for as long as adjustment is possible.

The programme accepts this. Selection attempts to identify candidates who trend toward the stable end of the spectrum, using the accumulated data of every previous induction as a predictive model. The model is imperfect. Some candidates who appeared stable are not. The programme has records it does not share with other Branches and outcomes it does not document in any form accessible outside Scarlet's own restricted archive.

Active strength at any given time: thirty to forty operatives. Small enough to maintain genuine elite standards; large enough to sustain concurrent operations across both Arbour's interior and the territory beyond its walls. Turnover is ongoing. Inductions run continuously to replace operatives who reach the end of their service. The programme does not refer to this as turnover.

### What the Enhancement Actually Is

At Stage Two and the threshold of Stage Three, Custodians gain two things. The programme has clean language for one of them.

The first: perceptual extension. Environmental reading sharpened beyond normal biological range — Aetheris concentration sensed before it becomes consciously legible, approaching presences registered through something closer to weather-sense than ordinary perception, structural instabilities in terrain or built environments felt rather than seen. Not a superpower. A deepened instinct that becomes reliable enough to trust tactically and consistent enough to deploy. It mirrors, from the outside, the same perceptual shift Aran experiences in the Free Territories — the Hum becoming a navigational tool, the land reporting things it shouldn't. The difference is that Custodians have a framework for it. The programme calls it convergence awareness in internal documentation. The framework keeps it operational rather than existential. This is what the programme selected the framework for.

The second: something harder to categorise. At Stage Three, some operatives begin to perceive things that exist at the boundary between this reality and the Convergence — not visions in the way Aran experiences them, but a quality of knowing that doesn't originate from sensory input. A sense of what is about to happen a breath before it does. An acute awareness of which situations have the thinness that precedes tears. The programme calls this an extension of convergence awareness and treats it as a tactical asset. Operatives who experience it describe it in the programme's language when they are asked to. Some of them experience it as something that no longer quite fits in that language. Most of them do not say so.

The parallel with Aran's experience is exact and unacknowledged. He has no framework. They have a framework designed to keep them functional. The framework and the absence of one produce very different people.

### What Happens When Custodians Deteriorate

When monitoring identifies progression toward Stage Four, the operative is informed. The programme has a specific protocol for this conversation — how to frame it, what language to use, what options to present. Voss delivers it personally for every operative she has inducted. She has delivered it many times.

The options as presented: continued limited deployment, working within reduced parameters as the condition is monitored. A support role within Scarlet Branch — training, logistics, analysis. Retirement to a classified medical facility for continued care and observation, with the assurance that the programme's obligation to its operatives does not end at the point of service.

The options as they actually function: continued deployment runs until Stage Four symptoms make field work unviable, at which point the decision is made for them regardless. Support roles are real; the programme finds genuine use for people who know what Custodians know and can still function without field deployment. Retirement means transfer to a classified Scarlet Branch medical provision — small, quiet, outside the city's standard healthcare infrastructure, staffed by practitioners who understand what they are looking at. Operatives who go there do not come back into active service. Most of them are not expected to.

The choice is real in the sense that the programme does not force an answer. It is an illusion in the sense that all three paths end in the same place, at different speeds, and the programme knows this and the operatives largely know this, and the conversation happens anyway because the form of it matters even when the content is managed.

The programme calls this giving operatives agency over the end of their service. Some of them find comfort in it. Voss delivers the conversation personally every time because she believes this is what the obligation requires of her. She has organised her psychology around the belief that the obligation is real. The organisation holds. It leaves a particular texture.

## Voss Shearwall

Branch head. Wolf, burnished onyx colouring, bearing that carries the specific quality of someone who has nothing left to prove to anyone and knows it.

Voss was an early inductee into the programme — among the first generation of Custodians to go through the controlled exposure process rather than encountering Aetheris incidentally in the field. She was an operative for many years before she led anything, which means she knows what the programme asks of its members from the inside rather than from a remove. She has held Stage Three exposure for longer than most. Whether this is because her biology is genuinely exceptional, because she has managed the exposure with the discipline of someone who has spent decades learning exactly what she's dealing with, or some combination of both, is not something she discusses.

She runs the programme with the particular authority of someone who went through it and came out the other side. When she selects a candidate, she is not making an abstract institutional decision. She is making a judgment about a person she will, in some cases, watch deteriorate. She has done this before. She will do it again. The programme requires it and she believes in the programme.

She does not know about the Twelve. She receives the directives that originate from them through the Outer Council channel and executes them with the same professionalism she applies to everything else. She has noted, over years, that some directives carry an unusual specificity — a quality of knowing things they shouldn't quite know, directing operations toward objectives that the Outer Council's visible intelligence shouldn't quite have. She has filed this observation and not acted on it. The programme has taught her, among other things, when not to look too closely at a source.

Her relationship to Aetheris is the longest and most intimate of anyone in Arbour who isn't one of the Twelve. She understands what it does. She does not know what it is.

## Operations

### Inside Arbour

Within Arbour's territory, Custodians operate in contexts Obsidian Branch cannot handle or cannot be seen to handle. Where Obsidian Branch's fifth division manages quiet removals through institutional mechanisms — career transitions, registry amendments, the paperwork of disappearance — Custodians manage situations that require physical presence, immediate resolution, and absolute deniability. The two institutions coordinate through a channel that does not appear in either Branch's official documentation.

Custodians are never uniformed within Arbour. They do not exist, officially, in any context where a resident might encounter them. What a resident experiences, if they experience anything at all, is simply a situation that resolved itself.

### Outside Arbour

The Badlands, the territory beyond Arbour's formal boundary, the wider continent — these are Custodian operational terrain in ways they are not for any other Branch. Obsidian Branch's authority ends at Arbour's border. Custodians' authority, such as it is, extends as far as the programme needs it to.

Outside the city, the Aetheris environment is different. The concentration varies by location. Active Installation sites produce the kind of intensity the programme uses in controlled induction, but uncontrolled, without the monitoring infrastructure, in terrain that Custodians must navigate with their own Stage Two or Three perception as the primary tool. This is where the enhancement earns its cost most clearly — and where the parallel with the Wayfarers who range the same territory, experiencing the same environmental pressure through entirely different frameworks, is closest and most unacknowledged.

## Inter-Branch Relations

Scarlet operates with more independence than any other Branch. Other Branches resent this and always have. The resentment is occasionally expressed through official channels — resource allocation disputes, jurisdictional complaints, the specific irritation of institutions that have to account for themselves to each other being refused equivalent accountability from Scarlet. Scarlet processes these complaints with consistent, courteous non-engagement.

The one inter-Branch relationship that functions differently is with the Twelve's conduit through the Outer Council — the channel through which Scarlet receives its actual operational direction. Voss does not think of this as an inter-Branch relationship. She thinks of it as her reporting line. She is not wrong about this in ways she would recognise.

## Atlas Faelan — What the Programme Did

In Act Two B of Book One, Custodians acting on Voss Shearwall's authorisation take Atlas Faelan — Wren Emberlain's partner — torture him, and leave him to be found. He dies from what is done to him.

The order Voss received came through the Outer Council channel with the unusual specificity she has learned not to question. The directive was to send a message to Wren — to make unmistakably clear that the investigation into the archive needed to stop. The method was intimidation. Voss authorised it as such: not execution, a warning delivered through the person Wren could least afford to lose.

Her operatives went further than instructed, or Atlas's body — already carrying two chronic conditions, already running on a system that Aetheris had been quietly accelerating — could not survive what was done to it. Either way, the outcome was not what Voss sanctioned. She sanctioned what led to it.

This distinction will matter. Voss is not a person who ordered an execution and is lying about it. She is a person who ordered something and did not account for what it would cost, in service of a directive whose real origin and real purpose she never knew. Cassan Vale gave her a target and a reason. The reason was not the real reason. The man who died was a musician with a chronic illness whose only crime was being loved by someone inconvenient to the Twelve's agenda.

Voss does not know any of this yet. She knows the operation exceeded parameters. She has filed that internally in the way the programme has taught her to file things that don't resolve cleanly, and moved on. The programme requires this of her and she has always been able to do it.

She will not always be able to do it. Her arc toward eventually becoming an ally to Wren and Aran is built on this foundation: the moment she understands what she was used for, what she was the instrument of, and what it cost. That knowledge — that she gave the order, that she was deceived about why, that Atlas Faelan died because someone above her wanted Wren to stop asking questions — is what cracks her relationship with the institution open. She does not switch sides because the Council is corrupt in the abstract. She switches because she discovers she was the specific, named instrument of something particular and wrong, and she cannot unknow it.

Her first scene with Wren, whenever it comes, carries this weight — known to the reader, unknown to both characters in full.
