## What It Is

The Golden Branch manages the flow of physical resources through Arbour — internal distribution of food, water, and materials across the city's tiers, and external supply chains that bring in what Arbour cannot produce itself. It is not armed. It does not enforce. It does not classify. What it does is know, with more precision than any other institution in Arbour, exactly what the city has, what it needs, and what the gap between those two figures actually looks like.

That knowledge is its power, and it is considerable. In a city that has survived for three centuries on the edge of what its systems can produce, the institution that controls the flow of physical goods and holds the real accounting of Arbour's resource position sits at the centre of everything, quietly, without the visibility that Obsidian's enforcement or Azure's technical authority commands.

## Internal Distribution

### What Golden Manages

Every physical resource that moves through Arbour's formal economy moves through Golden Branch's distribution infrastructure. Food produced by Verdant Branch's agricultural facilities reaches community distribution points through a logistics network Golden administers. Water allocation from the recycling system, materials for Azure Branch maintenance operations, consumables for Branch facilities across all tiers — the routing, the scheduling, the record of what went where and when, all of it is Golden's operational domain.

This makes Golden the body that decides, in practice, which districts receive their allocation first when supply runs short, which maintenance teams get the materials they requested and which wait, which community distribution points are resupplied on schedule and which are told to expect delays. The formal criteria for these decisions are documented. The discretion within those criteria is real and exercised daily.

### The Verdant Conflict

Golden Branch and Verdant Branch are in constant low-level conflict over resource allocation, and have been for as long as both have existed. The tension is structural rather than personal: the Branch that produces resources and the Branch that distributes them have fundamentally different interests and fundamentally different relationships to the same numbers.

Verdant's position is that it produces what it produces, and Golden should distribute it fairly according to tier allocation and need. Golden's position is that distribution is not a neutral act — logistics, infrastructure capacity, storage limits, and supply timing all affect what is actually possible, and Verdant's production figures do not account for the reality of getting goods to where they need to go. Each Branch has genuine leverage over the other. Verdant can adjust what it produces and when. Golden can adjust what it prioritises and how.

When Arbour's population experiences a resource shortfall, both Branches point at each other. The political blame game in scarcity situations is old enough to have its own established choreography: Verdant cites production figures that demonstrate sufficiency; Golden cites distribution constraints that explain the gap between those figures and what residents actually received. Both accounts are partially true. Neither is complete.

## External Supply

### What Arbour Cannot Produce

ARBOUR|05's founding population brought more knowledge than Cordis could sustain across three centuries of isolation. Some materials Arbour once had in stock have been consumed and cannot be replaced from local sources. Some compounds require production knowledge the Azure Branch has partially lost. Some categories of goods — certain medical precursors, specific technical components, food variety beyond what Verdant's controlled agricultural facilities can produce — exist in Arbour only because they come in from outside.

Golden Branch manages these external supply chains. Trade relationships with Wayfarer caravans for goods and agricultural variety. Contact with fixed settlements beyond Arbour's formal territory who produce or hold things Arbour needs. The occasional longer-range arrangement that Golden's official documentation describes in terms that emphasise legitimacy and obscure specificity.

### The Shadow Supply

Arbour's real external supply picture includes channels that do not appear in Golden Branch's official records. The shadow supply network — the informal, off-registry flow of goods through Wayfarer contacts, Badlands community traders, and figures like those in Jennifer Mosswood's chain — provides resources the official supply cannot reliably deliver, at prices and through arrangements that official documentation cannot acknowledge.

Golden Branch does not officially know about this. Its formal position, if asked, is that external supply is managed through documented trade relationships with verified counterparties, and that any unofficial supply activity occurring within or around Arbour's territory is a matter for Obsidian Branch rather than Golden.

This position is maintained consistently and is not quite true.

Within Golden Branch, one or two individuals are deeply embedded in the shadow supply network — connected enough that the network's actual flow runs partly through them, that they know figures and contacts that their Branch's official records don't contain, and that removing them from their positions would not merely inconvenience the shadow supply but break something load-bearing in how it functions. Their Branch colleagues do not ask about the specifics of how certain supply gaps get quietly resolved. Their superiors have learned not to need to. The arrangement works because everyone involved understands, without it ever being stated, that the official position and the operational reality are two separate things that are both necessary and must never be directly connected.

These individuals are unnamed for now — to be established when a plot thread requires a specific Golden Branch character with shadow supply connections.

## Internal Culture

Golden Branch is the most pragmatic institution in Arbour's power structure. It deals in material reality — weights, volumes, timelines, the difference between what the official supply figures say and what actually arrived — in a way that leaves limited room for ideology. Its members tend toward the specific and the numerical. They are the people who know that Arbour's food security is narrower than the Council's public communications suggest, that certain resource categories are on trajectories that will become serious within a generation, and that the shadow supply is not an embarrassing anomaly but a structural dependency.

This knowledge produces a particular worldview: unsentimental, clear-eyed about the gap between official positions and operational realities, and quietly comfortable with holding both simultaneously. Golden Branch members are not, by and large, idealists. They are the people who know what things actually cost and what the city actually has, and who have made their peace with the fact that the official accounting and the real accounting are not the same document.

This also makes them, in their own way, the Branch most aware of how precarious Arbour's position actually is. The Council's official communications project stability. Golden Branch's internal numbers tell a more complicated story, and its senior members read both versions every day.

## Relationship to the Twelve

The Golden Branch head does not know the Twelve exist. They receive directives through the Outer Council that occasionally carry the same unusual specificity Obsidian's senior figures have noted — instructions that seem to know more than the Outer Council's visible intelligence should allow, priorities that arrive already formed rather than emerging from deliberation. The Branch head has concluded that certain Outer Council figures have access to supply intelligence they don't share broadly, and has not pursued the question further. The work of actually managing Arbour's resource position is consuming enough to make metaphysical curiosity about the chain of command feel like a luxury.
