# The Branches of Arbour — Overview

*Lives in: World & Lore → Factions & Power Structures. This document covers the shared framework — origin, structure, inter-Branch relationships, and the brooch system. Individual Branch articles cover each in depth: see azure-branch.md, verdant-branch.md, obsidian-branch.md, scarlet-branch.md, golden-branch.md, violet-branch.md. The Ivory Branch is addressed in arbour-healthcare.md.*

---

## Origin

ARBOUR|05 launched with a crew. Not a population in the civilian sense — a crew: people assigned to roles the ship required, organised by function, bound to those functions by training, oath, and the practical reality that the mission's survival depended on everyone performing their part. Engineers maintained the ship. Agronomists managed food production. Security personnel maintained order. Medical staff kept the crew alive. Administrators coordinated resources. Cultural and communications officers managed the human dimensions of an interstellar voyage.

When the ship crashed and the founding generation had to build a city from the wreckage, they organised what they had — which was a crew structure, a set of institutional identities, and a population that already understood itself in terms of roles and functions. The Branches did not need to be invented. They needed to be adapted. What had been ship departments became city institutions, carrying their original mandates into a context those mandates were never designed for, and accumulating three centuries of additional purpose, ritual, and cultural weight on top of the founding framework.

The result is a system that is, in some ways, three hundred years old and, in others, still running on logic designed for a voyage that ended before anyone currently alive was born.

## The Six Branches

Arbour has six active Branches. A seventh — the medical Branch — retains its formal existence but has effectively ceased to function as a recruitment and training institution. The six active Branches are:

**Azure Branch** — engineering, maintenance, and technical infrastructure. The largest Branch by membership and the one most directly responsible for Arbour's physical survival. Its training is apprenticeship-based; its culture is the closest to the original crew structure; its authority over the city's technical systems is effectively total. See *azure-branch.md*.

**Verdant Branch** — food production, agricultural systems, and the biological closed loop that keeps Arbour's population fed. Manages the controlled agricultural facilities within and adjacent to the hull, the water recycling system's biological components, and the wider supply of food resources. In constant low-level conflict with Golden Branch over the distinction between production and distribution. See *verdant-branch.md*.

**Obsidian Branch** — enforcement, public order, and the surveillance infrastructure jointly maintained with Azure. Initiates postings, administers the detention facility in the hull's lower levels, and operates the quiet removal mechanisms that handle problems the formal system cannot publicly acknowledge. Five internal divisions, two of which are uniformed and visible, three of which are not. See *obsidian-branch.md*.

**Scarlet Branch** — the Custodian programme and military operations inside and outside Arbour's territory. The most classified Branch, operating with independence from inter-Branch accountability that other Branches resent and cannot formally challenge. Administers a programme whose full nature is known only to its own senior members and, above them, to the Twelve. See *scarlet-branch.md*.

**Golden Branch** — resource distribution and external supply chain management. Controls the flow of physical goods through Arbour's formal economy and manages the trade relationships through which goods enter from outside. Holds more knowledge of Arbour's real resource position than any other institution. See *golden-branch.md*.

**Violet Branch** — cultural mandate, performance authorisation, and the administration of Arbour's official cultural life. The youngest Branch, established to manage civil unrest through controlled cultural outlet. Nominates cultural figures for tier reclassification. Consistently underfunded relative to its mandate. See *violet-branch.md*.

## The Ivory Branch

A seventh Branch — the Ivory Branch — predates Violet and holds a council seat. Its formal mandate covers healthcare provision across Arbour's population. Its pipeline has effectively collapsed: too few senior members remain with the standing and knowledge to sponsor, examine, and induct new practitioners. What medical provision still exists in Arbour's upper tiers operates through informal arrangement rather than formal Branch membership. The Branch's name is on the records. The institution it names has not functioned properly for at least a generation. See *arbour-healthcare.md*.

## The Brooch

Every Branch member wears a brooch — a metal pin bearing the emblem of their Branch, presented at induction as part of the ceremony that marks the crossing from candidate to crew. The brooch is not optional and not informal. It is the visible, public declaration of Branch allegiance, worn on the person in daily life, and carries the full weight of what Branch membership means in Arbour's social fabric.

Seeing a brooch tells you immediately who someone is in Arbour's institutional landscape — which Branch, and therefore something about their training, their loyalties, their place in the city's power structure. A Branch member without their brooch is notable. A civilian wearing a Branch brooch they haven't earned is a serious infraction.

Each Branch's brooch carries the Branch's own emblem — a symbol specific to its identity and mandate, distinct from the others, worn in the Branch's associated colour where the metalwork allows it. The specific emblems are established through each Branch's own tradition and are part of the visual language any Arbour resident learns to read early. See *Open Follow-Ups* for emblem specifics pending a design pass.

The brooch also functions as a social signal between Branch members of different institutions. Inter-Branch relationships carry enough history — alliances, fault lines, long-running conflicts over resource and jurisdiction — that the brooch a person wears shapes how they are received before they have said anything. An Obsidian Branch pin in a Violet Branch gathering reads differently than the same pin in an Azure Branch facility.

## Inter-Branch Relationships

The Branches did not emerge from a blank institutional slate. They emerged from a ship's crew structure that already had its own internal hierarchies, alliances, and tensions, and three centuries of city life have added considerably to those foundations.

**Known alliances:**

Azure and Obsidian have historically cooperated most closely — technical infrastructure and security share obvious operational interests, and the Joint Surveillance Unit that both contribute to is the most visible expression of that cooperation. The alliance is functional rather than warm; Azure Branch's culture of technical precision and Obsidian Branch's enforcement culture produce different kinds of people who work well together because they need to rather than because they particularly like each other.

**Known fault lines:**

Verdant and Golden are in constant low-level conflict over resource allocation — the producer and the distributor have structurally different interests and each has leverage the other needs. The conflict has its own established choreography: predictable positions, predictable escalations, predictable resolutions that leave the underlying tension unchanged.

Scarlet's independence generates resentment across all other Branches without exception. A Branch that does not have to account for itself to peer institutions, that receives classified operational direction that other Branches cannot scrutinise, and that has military deployment authority that can theoretically override normal jurisdictional boundaries is an irritant regardless of whether it exercises that authority. Scarlet processes this resentment with consistent, courteous non-engagement.

Violet's chronic underfunding relative to its mandate is a source of tension with the Branches whose resource allocations were secured in earlier, more crisis-driven conditions. The resentment is quieter than Scarlet's generates — Violet is the youngest Branch and its mandate is the one most easily characterised as non-essential — but it is older and more thoroughly institutionalised.

## The Council and the Twelve

Branch heads sit on the Outer Council. Not all of them know the Twelve exist. The ones who do — and there are not many — have reached that knowledge through different routes and hold it in different ways. Most Branch heads execute policy they believe originates from the Outer Council's deliberative process, receiving directives that are occasionally more specific and more forceful than that process would naturally produce, and declining to examine why.

The Twelve's use of the Branch structure is consistent with how they use everything else in Arbour's institutional landscape: they do not need to own it, only to direct it. A Branch that believes it is executing Outer Council policy and is, in fact, executing Twelve policy is a Branch that requires no special management. The belief does the work.
