## What It Is

The Verdant Branch manages water and food — the two things no population can survive without. This makes it, in practical terms, the most powerful Branch in Arbour, regardless of what any formal hierarchy says. Whoever controls what people eat and drink controls something more fundamental than law or currency. The Branch understands this. The Council understands this. The arrangement between them is the foundation of Arbour's social control at its most basic level.

Its mandate covers the full lifecycle of both systems: water recycling infrastructure and distribution, hydroponic food production, cultured protein facilities, the seed vault, and the nutritional allocation system through which every registered resident receives their baseline food provision. It also covers, in official terminology, **Organic Cycle Completion** — the reintegration of biological matter, including human remains, into the water recycling system. This is common knowledge in the Sprawl. It is not discussed in polite company in the upper tiers, though the upper tiers drink the same water.

## Water

Arbour is a closed system. Every drop of water that arrived on ARBOUR|05 is still somewhere in the city — recycled, purified, redistributed, consumed, recycled again. Nothing leaves. Nothing is added from outside through official channels. The Verdant Branch manages this cycle.

Water quality varies significantly by tier — geothermal condensation water for Luminary (clean, neutral, exceptionally pure), progressively less purified recycled water through Meridian and into the Sprawl, where the minimal purification produces what residents call *tastes of the system*: flat, metallic, slightly warm, with an aftertaste that Sprawl residents stop noticing in childhood and visitors never forget. It is technically safe. The Branch's safety threshold is calibrated at a level that meets minimum standards after its highest-tier purification process. What meets that threshold after minimal purification is a question the Branch does not publicly examine.

The solar condenser systems — atmospheric water collectors that should significantly supplement recycled water supply — have been running at miscalibrated settings for generations, producing approximately 40% of their designed output. The Azure Branch has filed three reports recommending a full condenser audit and recalibration. The Verdant Branch has blocked all three on jurisdictional grounds. The actual reason is that a full audit would surface questions about why miscalibration this significant was never corrected and who is responsible for that failure. The condensers continue to run as they are.

## Food

The hydroponic systems that form the backbone of official food production have been expanded significantly from the original ark configuration — Verdant's primary institutional achievement over three centuries is scaling hydroponic output to feed a continental city. This expansion has come at the cost of variety. The original seed vault held thousands of crop varieties. The current hydroponic systems grow approximately forty — those with the highest caloric yield per square metre, the lowest maintenance complexity, and the easiest processing into standardised nutritional allocations.

The seed vault still exists, held in Verdant Branch secure storage. Most of what it contains has not been grown in over a century.

Cultured protein — laboratory-grown, nutritionally complete, texturally unconvincing — is the primary protein source across most tiers. Upper tier residents receive cultured protein that has been processed further, flavoured, textured, and presented as something approaching real food. The base product is identical. The processing is the privilege.

The standardised nutritional allocation distributed to every registered resident was last reviewed and updated 73 years ago. It does not account for the increased caloric demands of physical labour, which the Sprawl disproportionately performs. Verdant Branch's nutritional science team filed a report noting this discrepancy 31 years ago. The report was received, acknowledged, and filed. The allocation was not updated.

### The Unofficial Food Economy

Alongside the official system, a thriving unofficial food economy operates — most visibly in the Sprawl. Hidden gardens in forgotten maintenance corridors, under salvaged lighting rigs, in sealed sections of structural fabric. Tended by people who learned from people who learned from people, carrying horticultural knowledge that exists nowhere in the Verdant Branch's records. The produce is real food in a way that standardised nutritional allocation isn't — grown from seeds saved across generations, tasting of something the official system has largely forgotten how to produce.

Verdant Branch knows these gardens exist in general terms. It does not know their locations specifically, and it has found it more convenient not to look too hard. Pursuing hidden gardens would require Obsidian Branch involvement, would generate documentation, would raise questions about why Sprawl residents are growing food outside the official system, and would ultimately produce more political difficulty than the gardens themselves create.

## Internal Culture

The Verdant Branch is, institutionally, less outwardly visible than Azure or Obsidian but more present in daily life than either. Every meal, every drink of water, every nutritional allocation represents a Verdant Branch decision made at some level. The Branch's members tend toward the procedural and the administrative — people who manage systems and outputs rather than people who fix things when they break or enforce things when they're challenged.

This produces a specific kind of institutional culture: focused on output metrics, on allocation numbers, on the managed efficiency of a closed system that has been running for three centuries and must keep running. The gap between the numbers the Branch reports to the Outer Council and the numbers it holds internally — the unreported condenser failures, the caloric deficit in Sprawl allocations, the 73-year-old nutritional standards — is a gap the Branch has lived with long enough that it no longer registers as a gap. It registers as the ordinary distance between official reporting and operational reality, which is part of how every institution in Arbour functions.

## Inter-Branch Relations

The Verdant/Golden conflict is the most consistent and structurally embedded inter-Branch tension in Arbour. Producer and distributor have fundamentally different relationships to the same resources, and each has leverage the other needs. Verdant controls what exists; Golden controls who gets it. The conflict has its own established choreography and shows no signs of resolution because the underlying structural tension cannot be resolved without one Branch absorbing the other's function, which neither is willing to countenance.

The Azure Branch relationship is periodically contentious in a different register — the condenser dispute is the most visible instance, but the broader pattern is two institutions with adjacent technical domains and overlapping jurisdictional claims, each protective of its own operational territory.
