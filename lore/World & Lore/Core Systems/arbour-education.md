# Arbour — Education

*Lives in: World & Lore → Core Systems. Companion to Political Systems (Branch structure), Economy and Enforcement (Cyph, tier access), and World Systems (the maintenance civilisation, ritual engineering). The education body's name is flagged as open — see Open Follow-Ups.*

## The Education Body

Education in Arbour is administered by its own body — distinct from the Branches, though it feeds into them and coordinates with several. Its name is pending (see Open Follow-Ups). Its mandate, in formal terms, is the transmission and preservation of Arbour's accumulated knowledge across generations.

In practice, its function is narrower than that mandate sounds. Three centuries of degradation have reduced what the body holds and what it can reliably transmit. What it does well is maintain a **canon** — a fixed, authorised body of what is known and what is taught — and deliver that canon with consistency across its facilities. It does not generate new understanding. It does not interrogate the canon it holds. Its measure of success is fidelity: a student who completes the curriculum knows what they are supposed to know, in the form they are supposed to know it.

The *why* beneath the procedures was the first thing to go. What remains is form.

## What Is Taught, by Tier

### Luminary

The broadest curriculum. Students at this tier receive something that looks, from the outside, like a genuine education: history, governance principles, cultural literacy, natural philosophy, mathematics, the arts. It is wide enough to produce the impression of intellectual formation.

The history is the Council's version — coherent, authoritative, and shaped around the founding generation's founding decisions. The natural philosophy stops well short of anything that would produce independent understanding of how Arbour's systems actually function. The mathematics is sufficient for administration and accounting, not for the kind of independent analysis that might surface inconvenient conclusions.

What Luminary education is actually producing is socialisation. Students learn how to *be* upper-tier — how to speak, how to carry themselves in formal contexts, what cultural references to hold, how to read a room composed of people like themselves. The curriculum is the vehicle. The product is a person who fits.

### Meridian

Narrower than Luminary, more practically deep within its lane. Meridian education is explicitly vocational — students are tracked toward Branch employment or administrative roles early, and the curriculum is shaped around what those destinations require. A student tracked toward Azure Branch learns different material than one tracked toward the Tabularium, with less overlap between tracks than the unified curriculum framework implies.

The breadth present in Luminary is largely absent. History is compressed to the authorised summary. Cultural content is present but thin. What Meridian students receive in depth — the technical or administrative knowledge relevant to their track — they receive with genuine thoroughness. The education body takes its feeder role to the Branches seriously. A well-prepared Meridian student arriving at Branch entry level is useful.

What they lack is context for what they know. They have the procedure without the principle. This is not experienced as a lack, because there is no reference point for what the principle would feel like.

### Sprawl

Formal provision exists in official terms. In practice, it is sparse, inconsistently staffed, irregularly funded, and concentrated in the districts closest to Meridian's border. Significant portions of the Sprawl have no functioning education body presence at all. The body's official accounting does not reflect this; its district coverage maps show facilities that exist as buildings, not as reliably operating schools.

What formal Sprawl provision offers: basic literacy and numeracy. Enough to be legible to the system — to read a posting, to navigate a Cyph transaction, to complete the minimum documentation the Tabularium requires. This is the functional floor, and it is calibrated to that floor. Nothing in the formal curriculum is designed to produce a Sprawl student capable of upward movement; Branch employment requires qualifications the Sprawl curriculum does not provide.

Most of what the Sprawl actually knows is transmitted sideways — family to family, community to community, elder to apprentice — outside the education body entirely. This informal transmission is older than any formal provision, more trusted, and carries knowledge the official curriculum does not hold: Cordis plant medicine, off-grid engineering, the oral histories of the districts, the practical knowledge of how the city actually functions at ground level. None of it is recognised by the education body. All of it works.

## What Is Absent Everywhere

Certain things are not in the canon at any tier.

The real history of the crash — what ARBOUR|05 knew, what the founding generation chose, why the cascade happened — does not appear. The authorised history covers the arrival and the founding without the events immediately preceding them.

The other arks are not mentioned. There is no official position on whether other ships existed. The question does not arise because the curriculum does not raise it.

Aetheris — as a phenomenon, as a cause, as anything beyond the name Cordis Rejection Sensitivity in the medical section — is absent. CRS appears in health content as an environmental sensitivity condition. Its treatment is noted. Its cause, per the curriculum, is recycled air and atmospheric particulates.

Most significantly: the curriculum nowhere teaches students *how to understand*, as distinct from *what to know*. The framework for generating independent conclusions from first principles — what an earlier civilisation might have called critical thinking or scientific method — is not present as a named, taught skill at any tier. Luminary students are taught to reason within established frameworks. Meridian students are taught to execute within established procedures. The frameworks and procedures are not themselves subject to examination.

This is not a conspiracy. It is what happens when three centuries of transmission without renewal gradually compress a body of knowledge into its most repeatable, least generative form. The education body is faithfully transmitting what it was given. What it was given was already reduced. What it transmits now is reduced further. It does not know what it has lost, because the loss is in the gap between what it holds and what it no longer holds, and gaps do not announce themselves.
