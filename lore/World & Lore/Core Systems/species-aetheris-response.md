# Species Biology and Aetheris Response

*Lives in: World & Lore → Core Systems. Companion to Species & Physiology — Foundational (baseline biology), The Convergence / Aetheris (stage mechanics and physical rules), Aetheris Distribution and Exposure Geography (environmental factors), and the Scarlet Branch document (Custodian programme's operational data). The full mechanics are deferred to Technical Appendices #67 — Aetheris Anomalous Mechanics. This document covers observed patterns and in-world understanding only.*

---

## The Baseline Principle

Aetheris susceptibility is not a species trait. It is an individual trait that correlates — weakly, inconsistently, with significant overlap — with certain biological profiles that are themselves distributed unevenly across species.

This distinction matters because the Custodian programme has, on several occasions, attempted to use species as a screening criterion and found it clinically useless. A wolf and a fox and a capybara can all present Stage Two. A different wolf, a different fox, a different capybara can live in Badlands proximity for decades and present nothing. The species is not the variable. Something else is, and species is only a noisy proxy for it.

What that variable is remains incompletely understood. The programme has accumulated two centuries of observational data and arrived at a working model that its practitioners trust operationally and that its theorists know is insufficient as explanation.

---

## The Nervous System as the Interface

The working model is neurological.

Aetheris appears to interact with biological nervous systems at the level of sensory integration — specifically the systems responsible for assembling raw sensory input into a coherent model of the environment. Stage One is characterised by heightened sensory acuity; Stage Two by extended perception that reaches beyond biological instrument range; Stage Three by something stranger that the programme's language does not cleanly capture. Each stage is a modification to the same underlying system: the machinery that builds the organism's moment-to-moment picture of the world.

This points to a susceptibility profile that correlates with the architecture of that machinery. Nervous systems that integrate sensory data more extensively — that process more inputs, maintain more complex environmental models, sustain higher baseline attentional load — appear to offer more surface for Aetheris to interact with. Not more vulnerability in the sense of fragility. More interface. More available architecture for something to take hold in.

The practical consequence is that susceptibility clusters in profiles the programme describes, for want of better language, as *high-integration*: individuals whose sensory processing is already running toward the upper range of what their biology supports. This profile is not evenly distributed across species. It is also not exclusive to any species. Which is why species is a noisy proxy rather than a direct predictor.

## The Canid Pattern

The programme's data shows a statistically elevated representation of canids among Stage Two and Three presenters — foxes, wolves, coyotes, and related lineages appearing at higher rates than their proportion of the general population would suggest.

This is noticed. It is not explained.

Several hypotheses circulate within the programme's practitioner community. The most commonly cited: canid sensory integration systems, evolved for complex environmental tracking across variable terrain, happen to produce the high-integration profile at above-average rates within the species. The architecture that makes a coyote an effective long-range navigator, or a wolf effective at reading distributed pack behaviour across distance, may be structurally similar to whatever Aetheris uses as its interface. If so, the canid elevation would not be a special relationship between the species and Aetheris — it would be an incidental consequence of the specific neurological trade-offs canid evolution settled on.

This hypothesis is plausible. It is not confirmed. It does not explain the significant number of canids who show no susceptibility at all. It does not explain why other species with comparable sensory integration profiles — some birds, some mustelids, certain large felids — appear in programme data at rates closer to population average despite, in principle, sharing relevant architectural features.

The canid pattern is a real observation. Its cause is unknown. Practitioners who have served long enough to see the pattern tend to watch canid candidates with slightly more attention, which may itself be introducing measurement bias into data that will be used to confirm the pattern. Nobody has fully disentangled this.

## What Species Is Not

A few misconceptions the programme has formally or informally retired:

**Prey species are not protected.** Early Custodian doctrine assumed that prey species' nervous systems — wired for rapid external threat detection rather than extended environmental modelling — would show lower Stage Two rates. This did not hold. Prey species show elevated vigilance architecture, which the programme eventually recognised as a different flavour of high-integration rather than its absence. Some of the programme's most consistent Stage Two presenters over the years have been prey-lineage individuals.

**Size is not a factor.** Smaller species do not present differently at the stage level than larger ones, controlling for integration profile. The biological scale of the organism is irrelevant to the interface.

**Carnivore vs. herbivore diet history is not determinative.** What matters is the nervous system's architecture, not the metabolic context that architecture evolved to serve.

**Geographic lineage on Earth is not meaningful.** The colony ship carried populations from multiple lineages. Three hundred years on Cordis has not produced any differential adaptation that shows up in Aetheris response data.

## What the Programme Knows vs. What It Doesn't

**Known:** That susceptibility varies individually. That high-integration nervous system profiles show elevated risk. That canids present at above-average rates. That exposure environment interacts with baseline susceptibility — the same individual in a hot zone will progress faster than in a moderate-Aetheris environment. That psychological state at time of first significant exposure affects early presentation (an individual in sustained high-stress conditions may cross Stage Two threshold faster than the same individual in stable conditions, given identical exposure).

**Not known:** Why individual variation is as high as it is within species and profiles. Why some high-integration individuals never show any effect regardless of sustained exposure. Whether there is a threshold of integration beyond which susceptibility becomes near-certain, or whether it remains probabilistic at any level. Why the canid elevation exists specifically. Why some Stage Three cases progress rapidly and others stabilise for years, with no reliable predictor.

**Actively avoided knowing:** The programme has not, in its two-century operational history, formally studied the connection between susceptibility and the individual's subjective experience of the world — what they attend to, how they process uncertainty, whether their habitual mode of environmental engagement constitutes a kind of integration that isn't captured in standard neurological assessment. Several long-serving practitioners have formed private views on this. None have committed them to programme documentation, because the question points toward territory that is difficult to study without acknowledging that what Aetheris does to a nervous system may not be entirely foreign to what that nervous system was already doing.

## Wren Emberlain — A Specific Exception

Wren does not present Stage One or Stage Two markers despite sustained proximity to Aetheris-relevant environments across their adult working life. Their archival access to material concerning the Convergence and the Installations has been ongoing for ten years. The monitoring flag on their record, placed by the Azure Branch's Continuance Corps, was never triggered by CSD presentation.

Why Wren is unaffected is not established. Several explanations are structurally possible:

The memory reconsolidation procedure — forced recall under a destabilising agent, corrupting the targeted memory as it re-stabilised — may have produced an incidental modification to the specific sensory integration architecture that Aetheris uses as its interface. A procedure designed to edit what Wren remembered may have also, unintentionally, altered something about how Wren processes environmental input at the level where Stage One would begin.

Wren's integration profile may simply not present the architecture Aetheris interacts with. High-integration nervous systems are not the only kind of complex nervous system — there are other ways to be cognitively sophisticated that don't produce the specific sensory-assembly architecture the working model identifies as the susceptibility factor.

The Convergence, which directed the crash of ARBOUR|05 and has demonstrably acted with precision on individual humans before, may be leaving Wren specifically unaffected for reasons of its own. This explanation is the one nobody in the story's current frame of knowledge is equipped to formulate, because it requires knowing that the Convergence operates with that degree of intentional specificity on individuals — which is not currently known by any character.

What this means practically: Wren can go places and handle materials that would be operationally risky for Aran. Whether Wren will ever notice this immunity — have reason to register that they are not experiencing something others experience — is a scene-level question for drafting.

## Cordis Biology and Aetheris

Cordis evolved its own biosphere independently of Earth lineages. That biosphere has co-existed with active Penumbran Installations for longer than any record reaches.

What this means for Cordis-native species: unknown. The Custodian programme works almost exclusively with Earth-lineage populations. Cordis-native fauna exists in the Badlands and open ecology. Whether Cordis-native nervous systems interact with Aetheris differently — having had generations to develop alongside it — is a question nobody in Arbour's institutional structure has thought to ask, because the study of Cordis biology at that level of sophistication is not something Arbour's intellectual infrastructure currently supports.

This is available as a future thread. For Book One, it is background texture only.

**No sapient native life exists on Cordis.** The Penumbrans were the only people this system ever produced, and they are gone. Cordis's biosphere is real and distinct — complex fauna, some of it behaviourally sophisticated — but nothing person-level. Arbour's settlers are alone in that sense. The weight of the Penumbrans' absence is therefore total: no successor, no inheritor, no species that carries forward what they were.
