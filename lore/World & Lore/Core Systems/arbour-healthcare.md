# Arbour — Healthcare

*Lives in: World & Lore → Core Systems. Companion to Political Systems (Branch structure), Economy and Enforcement (Cyph, tier access), and World Systems (Aetheris, Atlas's condition). The medical corps is the Ivory Branch.*

---

## Part One — The Medical Corps

### What It Was

ARBOUR|05 launched with a medical division: trained physicians, surgeons, pharmacologists, and the equipment and supply chains to sustain them across an interstellar voyage. Medicine on the ship was not perfect, but it was coherent — a body of knowledge held by people who understood why the treatments worked, not simply that they did.

The founding generation carried that knowledge to Cordis. A Branch was formed. It had a name, a council seat, a mandate, and practitioners who had trained under people who had trained under people who remembered ship-level medicine.

### What It Is Now

Three centuries later, the Ivory Branch exists. The name is on the records. The council seat is filled. The mandate has not been formally revised.

The corps has been thinning for generations — each one training fewer successors than the last, because training requires time, equipment, stable supply chains, and practitioners skilled enough to teach. All of those have been in shorter supply with each passing decade. The gap between what the founding medical staff knew and what a current practitioner knows is enormous, and largely invisible to the practitioners themselves, because nobody alive has a reference point for full competence. They know what they know. They don't know what's been lost.

What remains: a small number of skilled practitioners, concentrated overwhelmingly in Luminary and upper Meridian. Diagnostic ability for conditions the corps has documented and named. Treatment protocols inherited and followed with care. Surgical capability for procedures that are recorded in legible manuals. A pharmacological supply that is shrinking — some compounds still produced in Arbour from maintained formulations, others no longer manufactured because the knowledge of how to make them degraded before anyone noticed it was going.

What is gone: the breadth of the original training, the understanding beneath the protocols, the ability to respond to conditions outside the documented set. A current corps practitioner follows procedure correctly and often effectively. Faced with something the manuals don't cover, they are as lost as anyone.

### Access by Tier

The corps' remaining practitioners are not distributed evenly.

**Luminary** has the most skilled practitioners, the best-maintained facilities, and the shortest wait times. Cyph cost is real but absorable at upper-tier allocation. Chronic conditions are managed with genuine continuity of care.

**Meridian** has adequate provision in the main districts — less skilled practitioners on average, longer waits, facilities that are functional but not well-equipped. Cyph cost is a meaningful consideration for lower Meridian households. Complex or rare conditions get referred upward; the referral may or may not be answered.

**Sprawl** has, in formal terms, almost nothing. A handful of corps-trained practitioners work in the Sprawl — some assigned, some who came voluntarily, some who were born there and returned. They are overextended, under-supplied, and working with equipment that has not been replaced in years. Cyph cost at corps facilities is technically scaled to tier, but the facilities that would honour that scaling are not in the Sprawl. For most Sprawl residents, the corps is the place you go when the situation has already become serious enough to make the journey to Meridian worthwhile — and by then, it is often very serious indeed.

## Part Two — Cordis Rejection Sensitivity

### The Diagnosis

Cordis Rejection Sensitivity — CRS — is a documented clinical condition in Arbour's medical corpus. It is real: a pattern of symptoms, a treatment protocol, a formal entry in the diagnostic manuals the corps trains from. Practitioners diagnose it in good faith. The treatment works on the presentation.

The corps does not know what causes it. Their working model is environmental — prolonged exposure to Cordis atmospheric particulates, recycled air contaminants, or some compound interaction between shipboard legacy biology and the local environment. The model is plausible. It fits the symptom pattern well enough that nobody has had reason to seriously revise it.

**What they observe and treat:**

Stage One presents as pressure sensitivity and dermal irritation — so common in Sprawl residents that the corps long ago stopped flagging it as clinically significant for that population. Officially attributed to air recycling quality. Symptom frequency in the Sprawl is not read as evidence that the environmental model might be wrong; it is read as evidence that Sprawl air quality is poor.

Stage Two — the opalescent shimmer entering peripheral vision — is the presentation that brings most patients in. The prescribed suppressant genuinely dulls the visual cortex's sensitivity. It works. Patients report improvement. The treatment is continued. The corps considers Stage Two a manageable chronic condition if caught and treated promptly.

What the corps cannot account for is why some patients deteriorate despite treatment, or why the condition in some individuals progresses to Stage Three regardless of compliance. Their documentation of these cases describes outlier responses to a known condition. The outliers are real. The explanation the corps holds for them is not.

**What the Council knows and the corps does not:** CRS is Aetheris exposure. The council does not share this. The corps has no reason to seek a different answer — the diagnosis works, the treatment works on the symptoms, and the cases that don't resolve are outliers in any diagnostic framework. The corps is not deceived in any single identifiable moment. It simply has never been told, and has never had cause to look.

## Part Three — Sprawl Medicine

### Self-Taught Healers

In the absence of corps provision, the Sprawl has produced its own medical culture — not as a deliberate alternative, but as a necessity that accumulated across generations into something with real coherence. Self-taught healers work from a combination of salvaged medical documents (patchy and inconsistent, some accurate, some outdated, some corrupted), accumulated community knowledge passed practitioner to practitioner, and empirical learning from working with what Cordis provides.

Their knowledge is uneven in ways they cannot always identify, because the gaps in the documents they learned from are invisible gaps — an absence doesn't announce itself. A Sprawl healer may be genuinely skilled in areas where their source materials were intact and has real blind spots where those materials were missing. They do not, by and large, know which is which.

They are also the people most available when something goes wrong. A Sprawl resident with a wound or a difficult birth does not have time to make the journey to a corps facility in Meridian. The healer who arrives is the healer who is there.

### Plant Medicine

Three centuries on Cordis has produced genuine pharmacological knowledge of local plant life. Sprawl healers know which compounds reduce pain, which slow bleeding, which help a fever break, which ease a respiratory crisis. This knowledge was arrived at empirically — by trying, observing, recording — and much of it works, within its limits.

Those limits are significant. Plant medicine handles the immediate and the surface. It can manage pain through an injury. It can slow bleeding long enough for the body to begin recovery. It can reduce inflammation, ease breathing, provide some relief from infection symptoms. What it cannot do is clear the infection itself. There is no Cordis equivalent of an antibiotic — no compound that eliminates a bacterial infection systematically, that reaches into the body and kills what is killing the patient. The corps has formulations that approach this; those formulations are not in the Sprawl and are increasingly difficult to produce even where they exist.

The practical result: a Sprawl resident injured badly enough to break skin is at real risk from what follows the injury, not the injury itself. The healer can stop the bleeding. If infection takes hold a week later, the options narrow quickly. Some patients recover on their own. Many do not. Infection is the quiet killer in the Sprawl — not dramatic, not acute, just a slow deterioration that the available medicine can slow but not reverse.

### The Knowledge Gap Running Both Ways

Sprawl healers know things the medical corps does not. Three centuries of close, empirical engagement with Cordis plant life has produced understanding that the corps, working from inherited Earth-derived frameworks, has never systematically sought. A corps practitioner handed a Sprawl plant remedy would not know what to do with it — not because it doesn't work, but because it falls outside their documented pharmacopoeia.

This is not widely acknowledged in either direction. The corps does not treat Sprawl medicine as a knowledge source. Sprawl healers do not expect the corps to.
