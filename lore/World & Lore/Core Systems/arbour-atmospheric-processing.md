# Atmospheric Processing

*Lives in: World & Lore → Core Systems. Companion to Arbour City Geography (the hull's relationship to the outside world), Arbour Power (the atmospheric energy harvesters), Arbour Healthcare (chronic exposure effects as a clinical problem), The Convergence & Aetheris (Stage One prevalence in the Sprawl), and Aetheris Distribution and Exposure Geography (debris field ground as a low-level exposure zone).*

---

## Two Kinds of Air

ARBOUR|05 was built to sustain life in the vacuum between stars. Every breath the founding generation took in transit was processed — scrubbed, balanced, conditioned, and recirculated through a system of extraordinary engineering maintained with the attentiveness of people who knew its failure was their death.

The crash ended that. Not because of a decision but because of physics: the aft section detonated, taking the primary processing infrastructure with it, and the survivors were on a planet with a breathable atmosphere. The founding generation's physicians confirmed within weeks that Cordis air produced no acute harm. Long-term questions were set aside for survival, which was the correct priority.

What remained of the processing system — intact in the surviving hull sections, functional at reduced capacity — was preserved by Azure Branch as part of their engineering inheritance. Three centuries later it still runs in the Luminary and upper Meridian: a conditioning system rather than a life-support one, filtering and stabilising air drawn through the hull-core's intake points. The air in the Luminary has a specific quality — processed, faintly sterile, the slightly flat texture of conditioned air. Long-term residents do not notice it. Visitors from outside do.

The Sprawl has none of this. It never did.

The Sprawl grew up in the open wreckage of the aft debris field — outside, exposed, built from salvaged crash material on ground that the intact hull never covered. There are no intake points, no filtration, no conditioning. The Sprawl breathes Cordis air directly, whatever comes with it, and whatever comes up from the ground beneath it. The processing system was never there to be degraded or lost. The question of Sprawl air quality is not about infrastructure failure. It is about what the ground the Sprawl is built on has always been off-gassing into open air.

## What Cordis Air Is

Breathable. The Wayfarer communities have lived in it for generations, ranging through open territory including Aetheris-affected zones, and manage. Cordis air on its own is not a meaningful health burden. The populations that live and die well outside any hull confirm this.

The Sprawl's air quality problem is not Cordis air. It is the ground the Sprawl is built on.

---

## The Debris Field

The Sprawl is built on the debris field — the scattered wreckage of ARBOUR|05's outer hull sections, cargo containers, and support structures that did not survive the descent intact. Three centuries of habitation have buried most of this wreckage under construction, compressed it under weight, and integrated it into the ground the Sprawl stands on. The ground itself is not natural Cordis terrain. It is a layer of Cordis soil over a substrate of crash debris over whatever was already in the eastern plain before ARBOUR|05 came down on top of it.

This substrate off-gasses.

Two distinct things come up from the debris field ground into Sprawl-level air:

**Crash particulates.** The wreckage includes materials that were never intended to be in contact with open atmosphere — hull composites, insulation compounds, structural alloys, and the remnants of systems whose chemical composition was designed for vacuum or controlled interior environments, not for three centuries of slow decomposition under weight and weather. What leaches out of this substrate into the ground, the groundwater, and ultimately the air at street level is a mixture that no one has ever fully characterised, because no institution has ever had the incentive to do so carefully. The Ivory Branch's degraded healthcare corpus does not include environmental toxicology at this scale. The Tabularium holds no comprehensive survey. What exists is generations of Sprawl healers noting that certain respiratory presentations cluster in certain districts, without being able to explain why.

**Aetheris seepage.** The crash disturbed the geology of the eastern plain in ways that extended below the surface. The 4.3-kiloton antimatter detonation that followed the jettison — and the 340% Aetheris spike it produced — interacted with whatever subsurface structures existed in this terrain. Three centuries later, the debris field ground is a slow, low-level Aetheris source: not the concentrated output of an active wellspring, but a diffuse seepage from disturbed subsurface geology that produces a measurable ambient Aetheris level at ground floor and below-ground in the Sprawl's oldest and densest districts. The aetheris-exposure-geography document notes slightly elevated eastern Sprawl readings; this is where those readings come from.

The two problems — particulate chemical exposure and ambient Aetheris seepage — are not separable by any diagnostic framework currently available in Arbour. Their symptoms overlap. The Ivory Branch practitioners who see Sprawl patients attribute the respiratory presentations to one or the other depending on which framework they were trained in, and neither attribution is fully wrong or fully right. The Sprawl healers who work without either framework describe what they see accurately and have no explanation for it that extends past the specific district and the specific building.

## Chronic Exposure Effects by Tier

### Luminary

The air is conditioned. The intake is filtered. The hull-core construction keeps ground-level off-gassing from reaching occupied floors — the Luminary sits high enough and is sealed well enough that debris field seepage is not a factor. Chronic exposure effects are negligible for atmospheric reasons specifically. (Other health disparities exist at the Luminary level, but they are not atmospheric in origin.)

Long-term Luminary residents have, over generations, acclimated to processed air in ways that make unprocessed Cordis air mildly unpleasant on first extended exposure — a quality they describe, when pressed, as a thickness or an earthiness that isn't quite comfortable. This is acclimation, not a symptom. It does not indicate harm.

### Meridian

Upper Meridian has partial conditioning coverage — the processing system extends here, with degraded but functional filtration at primary intake points. Ground-level debris field exposure begins at the Meridian's lower reaches, where the construction grade descends closer to the debris field substrate. Lower Meridian residents have measurably higher rates of the respiratory presentations clustered in the Sprawl, at lower prevalence.

The Meridian is also where the diagnostic gap becomes most visible as a social problem: Ivory Branch provision is present here, practitioners follow the inherited protocols, and the presentations they are seeing are consistent enough that several practitioners over the past century have formally noted a geographic correlation in their records. These notes are in the Tabularium's civil medical archive, unread and uncorrelated by anyone with access to the comparative data that would make their significance legible.

### Sprawl

The Sprawl breathes open Cordis air — which is fine — over a debris field substrate that off-gasses particulates and Aetheris seepage into street-level air continuously, in buildings that are poorly ventilated and overcrowded enough that what comes up from the ground has nowhere to go. The air in the lower floors of the Sprawl's oldest districts — Ashwick and its immediate neighbours, built directly on the most debris-dense ground — has a quality that residents describe variously as heaviness, staleness, or simply the way air smells in this part of the city. Newcomers from other districts notice it. Lifelong residents have stopped noticing it in the way that lifelong residents stop noticing anything persistent enough.

Chronic exposure effects in the Sprawl are real, compound, and poorly documented:

**Respiratory presentations** — chronic low-grade inflammation of mucous membranes, elevated incidence of persistent cough, reduced baseline lung function in adults who have spent their lives at ground level in the oldest districts. The Sprawl healers treat the symptoms. The cause is not in their framework.

**The scratch** — Stage One Aetheris exposure symptoms (the pressure in the skull, the contact-dermatitis sensation under the skin) are endemic in the eastern Sprawl's older districts. They are common enough to have a name. The official attribution — recycled air quality — is an artefact of the founding generation's explanation for the same symptoms in sealed ship air, repurposed without examination as Arbour's official position on a Sprawl phenomenon that has nothing to do with recycled air. The actual cause — ambient Aetheris seepage from the debris field substrate — is not in any official document. The cover story is accidentally almost accurate in form (something in the air produces the symptoms) and completely wrong in cause.

**Compounding with CSD progression** — residents who are Aetheris-susceptible and living in the Sprawl's ground-level Aetheris environment are subject to chronic low-level exposure rather than the episodic exposure of the Custodian programme or the incidental exposure of a Badlands visit. The effect of chronic low-level exposure on susceptible individuals is not characterised by any institution in Arbour. Cassan Vale has thought about this, in the context of his own research, and his current understanding is that chronic low-level exposure in susceptible individuals produces slower, more stable progression than episodic high-level exposure — but the progression is real, and its endpoint is the same.

## The Official Position

The Council's position on Sprawl air quality is that it reflects the challenges of dense habitation in a partially open urban environment, and that the healthcare provision available through Ivory Branch is adequate to address the presentations that arise. Neither claim is accurate, and the Council knows the first claim is not accurate in the specific sense of knowing that debris field off-gassing exists and has never commissioned a study of its effects.

The "recycled air quality" attribution for Stage One symptoms is the one piece of the official position that is in active institutional use rather than merely implicit. It appears in Ivory Branch training materials as the standard explanatory framework for the scratch's Sprawl prevalence — borrowed directly from the hull-core context, where recycled air quality is a genuine variable, and applied without examination to a population that has never been inside a sealed hull and has never breathed recycled anything. The Sprawl is open air. There is nothing being recycled. The explanation is not a lie constructed to deceive so much as an inherited framework applied in the wrong place by people who did not notice the mismatch, and then repeated by every subsequent generation of practitioners for the same reason.

This makes it, in some ways, harder to dislodge than an active fabrication would be. A lie can be caught. An inherited category error, embedded in training materials and repeated in good faith by practitioners who have no reason to question it, simply persists.

The attribution is also useful to the Council for a reason that is closer to active maintenance: it implies the problem is an infrastructure problem, not a geological one, and infrastructure problems can in principle be fixed. This means the Council can promise, periodically, to address air quality in the Sprawl without committing to any action that would require acknowledging what is actually happening below street level.
