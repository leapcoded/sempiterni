# Arbour — Law and Punishment

*Lives in: World & Lore → Core Systems. Companion to Economy and Enforcement (Cyph, posting system, Obsidian Branch), Political Systems (Branch structure, the Twelve), and the Tabularium (civil records). Infraction name for physical detention is flagged open — see Open Follow-Ups.*

---

## Part One — The Law

### Universal in Theory

Arbour has a single body of law. It applies to all registered residents regardless of tier, species, or Branch affiliation. The infractions are the same. The penalties are defined. The process — Obsidian Branch investigation, filing, the posting and its associated Cyph action, and where applicable physical detention — is the same process for everyone.

This is the official position and it is not technically false. The law does not have a Luminary clause and a Sprawl clause. The text is the same text.

### Tiered in Practice

What the law does not account for is everything surrounding it.

**Obsidian Branch discretion** — An investigation requires an Obsidian Branch officer to open it. What triggers an opening is formally defined, but in practice it requires a complaint, a flag, or an officer's own initiative. In Luminary, complaints against residents are less likely to be filed by neighbours who depend on those same residents' goodwill. In the Sprawl, Obsidian Branch operates with wider initiative and less accountability to the communities it polices. The infraction that gets filed in the Sprawl is the same infraction that gets quietly noted and set aside in Luminary.

**The ability to make a problem disappear** — A posting can, in some circumstances, be resolved before it fully propagates. A Cyph settlement, a formal retraction, an arrangement between parties that satisfies Obsidian Branch's threshold for closure. These mechanisms exist in the law. They require time, Cyph, and access to people who know how to use them. In Luminary, this is a known and frequently used route. In the Sprawl, the same mechanism exists and is functionally unreachable — by the time a Sprawl resident has navigated the Tabularium's filing process to initiate a retraction request, the posting has been on the community board for two weeks.

**Legal counsel** — There is no formal profession of advocate or lawyer in Arbour in the modern sense; the corps of trained legal practitioners that might have existed on the ship has thinned like the medical corps, and what remains is informal, expensive, and concentrated in the upper tiers. A Luminary resident facing a serious infraction has access to someone who knows how Obsidian Branch works from the inside. A Sprawl resident facing the same infraction has whatever they know themselves, whatever a community elder can tell them, and whatever unofficial knowledge has been passed sideways through the district.

**The appeals process** — Formally available to all. In practice, an appeal requires a written submission to a review body within a defined window, supported by documentation the appellant must source themselves, at a filing cost in Cyph. The window is the same for everyone. The cost is the same for everyone. The documentation requirements are the same for everyone. None of these things are the same for everyone.

The result: the law is universal. Its weight falls unevenly. A Sprawl resident and a Luminary resident committing the same infraction have, in practice, very different relationships to its consequences — not because the law distinguishes them, but because every layer of machinery surrounding the law does.

## Part Two — Infractions and Posting

### The Infraction Categories

Infractions fall into broad categories that determine the severity of posting duration and associated Cyph action. The exact names and sub-categories of these tiers are to be established in a dedicated naming pass — the shape is locked, the terminology is open.

**Minor infractions** — short posting duration, modest Cyph action. Public disorder, minor property interference, failure to comply with a Branch instruction. These are the most common postings and the most likely to be quietly resolved before the full duration runs.

**Significant infractions** — longer duration, more substantial Cyph action, job comms flag that persists beyond the posting itself. Fraud, more serious property offences, interference with Branch operations, organised unrest. These are the postings that compound into managed decline for residents without the means to absorb them.

**Serious infractions** — maximum posting duration and Cyph action alongside referral to physical detention. At this level, the posting itself is secondary to the detention; the public record follows the physical consequence rather than preceding it. Crimes against persons, major interference with Arbour's infrastructure, acts the Council classifies as threatening public order at a systemic level. This category is also where the law's flexibility is most visible: what constitutes "systemic threat" is defined by Obsidian Branch in coordination with the Council, and that definition has historically expanded to include organised political resistance, knowledge of certain suppressed facts, and the act of asking the wrong questions in the wrong places.

### Duration Bands

Specific duration bands are to be established during the Branch deep-dives once Obsidian Branch's internal structure is more fully detailed. The principle: duration scales with infraction category, modified by prior posting history. A first posting for a minor infraction is short. The same infraction for someone already posted within the past few years is longer. The system has a memory, and the memory compounds.

## Part Three — Physical Detention

### The Facility

Physical detention in Arbour takes place in a facility built into the lowest accessible levels of ARBOUR|05's hull — the ship's bowels, repurposed from original cargo and machinery bays in the founding generation and maintained at functional minimum ever since. It sits below the public levels of the hull-core, below the maintenance crawlways that Azure Branch uses, in the part of the ship that requires a deliberate descent to reach and that no one with alternatives ever visits.

The facility is administered by Obsidian Branch. Its physical infrastructure is technically within Azure Branch's maintenance jurisdiction, and the jurisdictional overlap has never been formally resolved — it has simply been left unresolved, which suits both Branches well enough. Obsidian Branch runs the facility without needing to account for its condition to anyone; Azure Branch is not responsible for what happens inside a space it doesn't claim to operate.

The conditions reflect the maintenance it receives, which is minimum. The hull at this depth holds temperature poorly — cold in Cordis's cold seasons, stuffy in its warm ones. Sound does not travel easily upward through the hull's construction. What happens in the facility stays in the facility in a structural sense: the architecture makes it so before policy ever needs to address it.

### Who Goes There

Physical detention is formally reserved for serious infractions. In practice, the same discretionary gap that shapes everything else in Arbour's legal system applies here too. Residents who cannot make a serious infraction disappear through settlement or connection are more likely to end up detained. Residents who ask questions the Council does not want asked, who organise in ways the Obsidian Branch classifies as systemic threats, who hold knowledge that is inconvenient — these residents find their infractions categorised at a level that justifies detention, regardless of what the underlying act actually was.

### Duration and Release

Detention has formal duration bands tied to infraction category. Release returns the resident to the Cyph system and the public record. The posting associated with the infraction persists after release — the duration of the posting is not reduced by time served. A resident leaves detention and returns to a community where the posting is still running, the job comms flag is still active, and the Cyph action has continued to compound while they were inside.

Some residents do not return. The official record in these cases documents a natural death, a medical event, or in some cases simply notes that the resident's registry entry has been suspended without further annotation. These records are filed by Obsidian Branch and processed through the Tabularium. They are publicly requestable, as all ARC records are. They are also written by the same institution that made the person disappear.
