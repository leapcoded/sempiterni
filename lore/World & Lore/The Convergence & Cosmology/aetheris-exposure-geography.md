## How Aetheris Distributes

Aetheris is not ambient in the way atmosphere is ambient. It does not spread uniformly across Cordis's surface as a background condition of the planet. It concentrates.

The primary concentrators are Penumbran Installations — structures built to generate, store, or channel Aetheris that have been operating in various states of function and decay for longer than any record reaches. Active Installations produce and sustain local concentrations. Dormant Installations retain residual charge that bleeds at a lower rate. Destroyed or structurally compromised Installations release what they held in unpredictable dispersal events, sometimes briefly spiking exposure across a wide radius before the field collapses.

The practical result is that Aetheris exposure across Cordis is a function of Installation density and activity state, not planetary geography. Two sites a kilometre apart can have wildly different exposure levels if one is near an active Installation and the other is not. This is why Badlands communities have learned, empirically, that certain landmarks are to be avoided — they are mapping Installation activity without knowing that's what they're mapping.

Secondary concentrators are less well understood. Certain geological features appear to channel or retain Aetheris in ways that don't map cleanly onto known Installation positions — narrow valley systems, specific mineral compositions in rock formations, underground water features that run near old Penumbran construction. The founding generation documented several of these anomalies in early survey records. Most of that documentation is buried in the same pre-Tabularium archive layers that hold the A05-MED records.

## Cordis as a Whole

At the planetary scale, Aetheris is not evenly distributed between the two main landmasses.

Arbour's continent has significantly higher Installation density than the Wayfarer continent. This is not coincidence — the Convergence selected Cordis as a destination, and the specifics of that selection almost certainly include the concentration of Penumbran infrastructure on one landmass over another. Whether the Wayfarer continent had its own Installation network that was more completely destroyed, or whether Penumbran civilisation on Cordis was primarily continental in its distribution, is not established in any surviving record.

The practical consequence: the Wayfarer continent has low background Aetheris, with occasional anomalous pockets. Wayfarer communities have developed almost no institutional understanding of Aetheris as a force, and almost no tradition around Installation avoidance, because sustained Installation proximity is not part of their landscape. The oral tradition that has built up around Aetheris in Badlands communities on Arbour's continent has no equivalent in the east. A Stage Two presentation among Wayfarer-born individuals is genuinely rare, and when it occurs, the available interpretive frameworks are religious or medical rather than the pragmatic-avoidance knowledge of Badlands communities.

The ocean between the two continents is low-exposure. The known underwater geography does not include significant Installation sites — or if it does, none have been located, and no surface-level exposure events have been documented by crossing groups across generations of ocean transit. Crossing is dangerous for reasons that have nothing to do with Aetheris.

## The Badlands: Variable and Hot

The Badlands contain the highest concentration of Aetheris on Arbour's continent and probably on Cordis overall.

The distribution is not uniform. The Badlands are not a single high-exposure zone — they are a landscape of varying intensity, with dormant stretches of relatively low exposure between clusters of active or semi-active Installation sites. What Scarlet Branch's operational data has mapped over two centuries of Custodian deployment is not a single field but a terrain of islands: elevated zones around active sites, lower channels between them, specific corridors where travel is possible without significant cumulative exposure, and specific zones where even short-term proximity produces observable effects in susceptible individuals.

### Hot Zones

Hot zones are defined operationally as areas where sustained presence — days to weeks — produces measurable effects in individuals who would not otherwise present Stage Two markers. They are not universally dangerous to everyone in them: the established fact that Aetheris exposure does not affect everyone equally holds here as strongly as anywhere. But the threshold for effect is lower in hot zones, and the progression, once begun, is faster.

Hot zones cluster around:

- **Active wellspring Installations** — structures generating Aetheris outward rather than containing it. The outer-system Installation where the survey crew harvested before the crash was a wellspring type; the Badlands contain several. These are the sites Scarlet Branch has most carefully claimed and monitored, and the sites Badlands communities most reliably avoid.
- **Nexus structures** — larger Installation complexes where multiple functional elements intersect. The Penumbrans appear to have built integrated systems rather than isolated structures; where these integrations remain partially active, the combined output exceeds any single element.
- **Collapsed containment sites** — Installations that have structurally failed and are bleeding their stored Aetheris into the surrounding terrain without any of the directional structure a functional Installation would impose. These are unpredictable and tend to produce the strange anomalous effects — Aetheris behaving in ways that don't fit the pattern of sustained exposure, that seem almost reactive rather than passive — that make certain Badlands regions feel wrong even to people who have never heard Aetheris named.

### The Moderate Corridor

Between the hot zones, the Badlands have a moderate exposure level — elevated above Arbour's background, but below the threshold for rapid Stage Two development in most individuals. This is the terrain Aran has ranged for two decades. His exposure here is chronic and low-level rather than acute: the kind of slow accumulation that primed his biology over years, making him susceptible to the click when the Convergence created the conditions, without pushing him through Stage Two presentation on its own.

Experienced Badlands navigators — Aran's caravan type, long-range scouts who know the terrain by accumulated observation rather than Scarlet Branch documentation — have learned the moderate corridor implicitly. They follow routes that avoid the hot zones without knowing they're doing it, reading Aetheris concentration through the Hum as a directional signal. This is precisely the navigational skill that makes Badlands-experienced individuals valuable to the Custodian programme's induction process, and that makes Aran functionally invisible to Scarlet's monitoring: he moves through the terrain in a way that produces no visible Stage Two presentation, because he has never sustained contact with a hot zone long enough to push past his own threshold.

## Arbour: Low Background, One Anomaly

Arbour's city proper has low Aetheris exposure by the Badlands' standards. It is not zero.

Two sources contribute to the city's background level.

First, the debris field. ARBOUR|05 struck terrain that was not empty. The eastern margin of what is now Arbour's Sprawl — the debris field's own asymmetric eastern concentration — came down on ground that had Penumbran-adjacent subsurface structure: old construction, old infrastructure, things the founding generation documented in early survey passes as "ground anomalies" and "subsurface interference" without knowing what they were. This material bleeds low-level Aetheris. Not enough to concentrate, not enough to produce Installation-adjacent effects in ordinary proximity. Enough to explain why CSD cases exist in a city that has spent three hundred years insisting the Badlands' dangers stop at its boundary.

Second, the Frames site. The solar collection arrays west of Arbour were not placed where they were placed by coincidence. The founding generation chose open terrain, chose proximity to the city but beyond the debris field's interference — and in doing so, chose a location that sits above a low-grade subsurface Installation. The original survey records that established this are among the pre-Tabularium material nobody has assembled correctly. Functionally: the Frames site has moderate local Aetheris, elevated above the city's background. Not a hot zone by Badlands standards, but enough that Cassan Vale built his research facility there rather than somewhere else. The access tunnel to the hull was practical necessity. The location was always the point.

Within Arbour, Aetheris exposure is not evenly distributed. The Sprawl's eastern concentration — the debris field ground — has slightly higher background than the Meridian or Luminary tiers, whose hull-core construction provides incidental shielding the Sprawl-level buildings don't replicate. This is not dramatically different. It is enough that CSD cases present at a slightly higher rate in the eastern Sprawl than in other districts, a pattern the Ivory Branch corps notes in its clinical data without having a framework to explain it.

## Historical Shift: Intensification

The Aetheris field on Cordis has not been static across the three hundred years since the crash.

The pattern that emerges from Scarlet Branch's cumulative operational data — never assembled into a single analysis, existing in fragments across Custodian deployment records that span two centuries — is one of slow intensification. The hot zones are not hotter than they were at founding; the installation sites Scarlet has monitored are not individually more active. What has changed is the field's coherence: the way Aetheris concentrations in different parts of the Badlands relate to each other, the way the moderate corridors have slowly narrowed, the way the anomalous reactive effects — Aetheris that seems to respond to the presence of susceptible individuals rather than simply exposing them — have become more frequent.

The intensification is not dramatic on any human timescale. It is the kind of shift visible only when you hold two centuries of deployment data side by side and notice that the maps of safe routes have been redrawn twice.

What is driving it is not established. Three candidates present themselves:

The crash itself disrupted subsurface Penumbran infrastructure in ways the three hundred years since have been slowly resolving — the Installations re-integrating, damaged containment structures resealing or continuing to fail, the field re-finding whatever coherence it had before a colony ship hit the continent at fractional interstellar velocity.

The Convergence's own influence is increasing. If ARBOUR|05 was brought to Cordis because this is where the Convergence needed it, then the conditions that required that act may be developing toward something — a threshold, a readiness, an event that the Convergence is positioning for. The intensification of the Aetheris field is not background noise. It is preparation.

Both simultaneously.

No character currently holds enough of the picture to distinguish between these explanations. The intensification is the fact. Its cause is unknown. What is functionally certain: the rate of Stage Two presentation in Arbour's population has slowly increased across the last two generations, and the Custodian programme's candidate pipeline from the Badlands has become more productive, and Cassan Vale has built his facility and launched his research at precisely the point in the intensification curve where Stage Three subjects have become available in sufficient numbers to study.

He did not create the conditions for his research. He noticed them arriving.

## Population Effects by Zone

**Luminary and Meridian (hull-core construction):** Lowest effective exposure in the city. CSD cases exist but are not common. The Ivory Branch corps treats them as idiopathic — the clinical term for "we don't know why this happens to some people." Suppression available through formal Ivory Branch provision. Stage Three extremely rare and, when it occurs, rapidly filtered toward Cassan's facility through mechanisms Voss does not know about.

**Sprawl (debris field ground, eastern concentration):** Slightly elevated background. CSD cases present at a higher rate, diagnosed inconsistently, treated primarily through self-supply or the Margin's medication network. The correlation between eastern Sprawl residence and CSD presentation is real and unexamined by any institution.

**The Frames perimeter:** Moderate exposure. Workers on the surface arrays experience chronic low-level Aetheris that the Ivory Branch corps, if it ever examined them, would recognise as consistent with early Stage One markers. They do not examine them. The workers are Sprawl-tier; formal provision is not their resource. Cassan's facility beneath the arrays operates in a higher local field — deliberately selected, operationally significant.

**Badlands moderate corridor:** Chronic low-to-moderate exposure across years or decades. The terrain where Stage Two development is possible but not guaranteed — where susceptibility, not exposure alone, determines outcome. The population Scarlet's monitoring is watching.

**Badlands hot zones:** Acute exposure risk. Stage Two presentation in susceptible individuals can occur within weeks of sustained proximity. Not survivable at Stage Three without institutional support. The population the Custodian programme draws its most advanced candidates from, and the population Cassan's second sourcing stream reaches.

**Ocean crossing:** Effectively zero Aetheris exposure. Wayfarers who cross are not changed by the crossing itself. The danger of the ocean has nothing to do with what the Badlands offers.

**Wayfarer continent:** Near-zero background with anomalous pockets not yet mapped by any systematic survey. Stage Two among the Wayfarer-born is rare enough to be treated, when it occurs, as individual aberration rather than environmental exposure pattern.
