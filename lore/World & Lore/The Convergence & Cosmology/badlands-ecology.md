# Badlands Ecology

*Lives in: World & Lore → The Convergence & Cosmology. Companion to Aetheris Distribution and Exposure Geography (the hot zones, moderate corridor, and collapsed containment sites that shape the ecology), Arbour and the Badlands (human relationship to the territory), The Installations (the structures the ecology organises itself around), and Arbour Healthcare (Sprawl plant medicine as the knowledge transfer point between Badlands ecology and the city). Also relevant to Aran Sunderwood's character documents — he has ranged this terrain for over two decades and reads it with a competence that the Badlands has shaped.*

---

## A Note on Cordis

The Badlands is one region of one continent on a planet with the same ecological range as any world with varied geography, latitude, and distance from its suns. Cordis has lush zones, wet zones, temperate zones — the full spectrum. The twin suns are far enough from Cordis that their combined output produces warmth and a distinctive light quality rather than an arid scorch across the board. The Badlands is arid because of its specific geography — elevation, rain shadow, geology — not because Cordis is a desert planet. This document covers the Badlands specifically. Communities elsewhere on Cordis exist in substantially different ecological conditions, to be established when those regions are developed.

## The Landscape

The Badlands is not dead. That is the first thing a newcomer gets wrong — expecting something blasted, sterile, the visual language of catastrophe. The terrain is harsh: dry and rocky at the far west where the elevation rises, transitioning through a zone of open, wind-scraped ground in the moderate corridor that Wayfarer caravans and Aeolian scouts have ranged for generations, with patches of genuine productivity wherever water finds a way through fissured rock or where the ground dips low enough to hold it. The twin suns dry the surface fast and the soil is thin in most places. Life here is not lush. It is persistent.

The scars are geological rather than biological — the legacy of an impact event in the deep past, not the crash of ARBOUR|05 (which came down on Arbour's continent, far to the east). Fractured rock shelves, dry riverbeds that fill seasonally and recede, ground that tilts and drops in ways that make traverse genuinely effortful. The elevated rocky margin at the territory's eastern edge — the transitional zone between Arbour's nominal boundary and the Badlands proper — gives way westward to more open, lower terrain, and it is in this lower terrain that the moderate corridor's ecology is most active.

Harsh but alive. That is what it actually is.

## What Aetheris Has Done

Three centuries of elevated Aetheris in the Badlands have not produced a visually alien landscape. There are no glowing plants, no obviously mutated creatures, no single dramatic transformation that would announce itself to a first-time visitor as wrong. The changes are subtler than that, and more unsettling because of it.

The core effect is **unpredictability**. Cordis life in the Badlands does not behave or present the way comparable life in lower-exposure territory does — not reliably. Most of the time it does. The plant by the dry riverbed looks like the plant by the dry riverbed should look, grows where it should grow, does what it does. The animal on the ridge behaves like the animal on the ridge behaves. And then, occasionally, it doesn't. Not in ways that follow a consistent alternative logic. In ways that are simply off — that don't quite fit the pattern the last hundred encounters established.

A plant that should be dormant in dry season is not. A predator that should avoid open ground uses it. A flowering cycle happens twice in one season, or not at all, in a stand where it has been reliably once for thirty years. An animal that should be solitary is found in a group; a group species is found alone. Colouration that is almost right, almost matching the baseline, but not quite — a saturation that is slightly too deep, a marking that is slightly too symmetrical, a skin tone that shifts at the edge of perception in certain light.

None of these are, individually, conclusive. Any single instance could be individual variation, a bad year, a local anomaly. It is the accumulation — the frequency with which something is slightly off, the pattern of unpredictability itself becoming the reliable feature — that marks Badlands ecology for what it is. Experienced Badlands travellers know this. They do not trust a baseline. They do not assume that a species will behave consistently just because it has behaved consistently so far. They carry that calibration in how they move through the terrain, in how they approach something familiar, in how much space they give to a creature they've seen a hundred times before.

Aran has this calibration. It is one of the things he is best at. He does not have a name for what produces it — the Hum as a navigational signal, yes, but the broader ecology of unpredictability is just the Badlands to him, absorbed through two decades of ranging. He does not know that what he has learned is, in part, Aetheris literacy — the accumulated reading of an ecosystem shaped by a force he has no framework for. He just knows not to assume.

## Near Hot Zones

Close to active wellspring Installations and collapsed containment sites, the ecology intensifies. Not in species diversity — the opposite, in most cases. The hot zone environments are sparse, as if the Aetheris concentration has narrowed the range of things that can survive in it rather than expanded it. What survives there is highly Aetheris-tolerant by definition, and has had three centuries to diverge in that direction.

The plants at the edge of hot zones show the most visually legible changes: thickened structures, unusual mineral uptake producing coloration that reads as wrong without being dramatic — a reddish-grey at the leaf margins, a sheen on certain surfaces that doesn't match the light correctly. The animal life near hot zones is reduced to things that either avoid the field actively (read it well enough to stay at its edge) or no longer respond to it the way unexposed animals do. Both categories behave unpredictably by ordinary ecological standards. The former because they are responding to a signal most animals don't have access to. The latter because what remains of their ordinary behavioural programming has been disrupted in ways that produce inconsistent output.

Badlands communities avoid hot zones practically. The knowledge is oral: this area is wrong, stay clear, and if you must pass through do it quickly and watch for the specific signs (the thickened margin plants, the absence of ordinary animal traffic, the quality of the Hum for those who can read it) that mark the boundary. Custodians move through hot zones with equipment and programme training. Ordinary travellers don't, if they know what they're doing.

## The Moderate Corridor

The moderate corridor — the lower-exposure territory between hot zones that constitutes the most ecologically complex zone in the Badlands — is where the ecology is most productive and most varied. This is Aran's primary ranging terrain. It is also where the Sprawl's plant medicine knowledge comes from.

The corridor's ecology is shaped by the same Aetheris-driven unpredictability as the rest of the Badlands, but at a level where the unpredictability operates around a stable-enough baseline that the ecology supports real diversity. Plants here have properties that lower-exposure equivalents don't — chemical profiles that have diverged over three centuries of Aetheris exposure, in directions that Sprawl healers and Badlands communities have mapped empirically without understanding the mechanism. A plant that manages pain. A plant that slows bleeding. A plant whose antimicrobial properties exceed anything Arbour's Ivory Branch corpus documents, because Ivory Branch has never studied it. This knowledge is practical, accumulated, and held entirely outside the official medical system — which cannot acknowledge what it doesn't know exists.

The corridor's animal life is the most ecologically significant and the least predictable. Prey species use the terrain in ways that don't fit standard flight patterns; predators have developed sensitivities that make them read as uncanny to someone who knows animal behaviour but not the Badlands specifically. Whether these sensitivities are Aetheris-mediated — whether certain animals in the moderate corridor have developed something adjacent to what the Custodian programme calls Stage One sensitivity — is a question nobody in Arbour has formally asked. Cassan Vale has thought about it. He has not had opportunity to study it directly.

## As Resource

The Badlands provides things the city cannot produce internally and the official supply chain cannot acknowledge sourcing externally. The resource extraction infrastructure Golden Branch runs through its shadow supply network draws on Badlands mineral deposits and specific biological material from the corridor's ecology. What the workers extracting these materials know about the ecology they are working in varies — some are experienced Badlands-adjacent people with real practical knowledge, some are Sprawl residents given minimal briefing and sent into territory they don't understand.

The plant medicine knowledge is a different kind of resource — one that flows into the city through informal channels rather than official ones. Sprawl healers who have contact with Badlands communities, or who have ranged the moderate corridor themselves, carry knowledge of specific plants and their applications that Ivory Branch does not hold. This knowledge is not systematised. It exists as personal expertise, passed person to person, occasionally written into private records that are shared carefully. It is real medicine — effective for pain, surface wounds, and certain infections in ways Ivory Branch provision in the Sprawl does not match — and it is completely invisible to the official healthcare system.

This is the knowledge gap that runs both ways established in the healthcare document: Sprawl healers know things the corps doesn't, and the corridor ecology is where most of that knowledge originates.

## As Hazard

The unpredictability cuts both ways. A plant that has useful properties under normal conditions has different properties when its chemistry has shifted in an Aetheris-influenced direction that the usual identification markers don't fully capture. A preparation that works reliably becomes unreliable if the source plant is from a patch closer to a hot zone than the harvester realised. Badlands communities carry a body of negative knowledge alongside the positive — what not to use, what to treat with caution, what looks like something useful and isn't — that is as hard-won as the positive knowledge and as invisible to anyone outside that tradition.

The animal hazard is less about danger from specific creatures and more about the unpredictability problem applied to threat assessment. An animal that should flee doesn't. An animal that has always been docile isn't. An animal that has never been observed in this part of the corridor is here, behaving in a way that doesn't match anything the established knowledge base covers. In low-exposure Cordis territory, the heuristic of "I know this species and it usually does this" is reliable enough to act on. In the Badlands, that heuristic is useful background and insufficient guide.

Aran knows this as deeply as he knows anything. He has not been badly wrong in the Badlands in over a decade. This does not mean he has stopped expecting to be.
