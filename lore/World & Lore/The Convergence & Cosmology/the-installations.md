---

## The Category Term

**Installation** is the universal term for Penumbran structures across all communities — Arbour's institutions, Wayfarer oral tradition, Badlands communities who have lived alongside them for generations. No community uses a different primary term. No community has a word that covers only some Installations and not others. The category is, in that sense, unusually stable: the thing itself demands a name, and every community that has encountered it has independently converged on roughly the same solution.

What "Installation" covers is not a consistent type of structure. It covers everything the Penumbrans built that survives and has been identified. Reliquaries and observatories and habitation sites and wellsprings are all Installations. A structure whose purpose has not been determined is an Installation. A structure whose purpose was understood once and has been forgotten is an Installation. The category term is not a description of function. It is an acknowledgement that something was deliberately made, by the Penumbrans, and is still here.

Not every Installation is identified. Not every identified Installation has a determined subtype. The four subtypes below represent the current state of understanding — three centuries of Arbour scholarship, two centuries of Custodian operational data, and the accumulated observation of Wayfarer and Badlands communities whose practical knowledge of specific sites often exceeds anything in the Tabularium. It is entirely possible that further subtypes exist. It is certain that individual Installations contain elements that fit none of the current categories cleanly.

## Physical Characteristics

Installations are not uniform in appearance, scale, or material. What they share is not a consistent aesthetic but a consistent relationship between structure and function that, once you have seen several, produces a recognisable quality — a sense of intentionality in every proportional decision, a geometry that does not feel accidental even when the purpose behind it is completely opaque.

**Scale** ranges enormously. Some Installations are smaller than a room; some occupy terrain measurable in hundreds of metres. The scale does not reliably indicate subtype or importance. A wellspring Installation may be compact; a reliquary may be vast. Size appears to have been a functional decision rather than a prestige one, which suggests the Penumbrans did not use scale the way human civilisations typically use it — as a signal of significance.

**Material** is consistently non-local in ways that matter. Installations in the Badlands are not built from Badlands rock, or not only. The primary structural material is a composite that appears across all confirmed Installation sites regardless of geography, suggesting either centralised fabrication or a material science that could be replicated consistently across the civilisation. Three centuries of Arbour scholarship has characterised this material without understanding it: extremely high structural integrity, anomalous thermal properties, specific acoustic behaviour that is not a by-product of the geometry but appears to be engineered into the material itself.

The acoustic behaviour is significant. **Sound moves through and within Installation spaces differently than it moves through human-built equivalents.** Not dramatically — not in ways that would immediately announce themselves to a first-time visitor. Subtly. A voice carries further than it should. Certain frequencies resonate in ways that don't match the geometry. Silence in an active Installation has a texture that silence in a dormant one does not. This is not incidental. The resonant layer of Penumbran language — the formal, ritual, possibly binding register that is built into the structures themselves — uses the acoustic properties of the material as part of its grammar. The Installation is not a container for the language. The Installation is the language, in part.

**Orientation** is consistent within each Installation but not consistent across them. Installations are not oriented toward each other or toward any single astronomical reference point. They are oriented toward something, individually — the geometry of each site makes clear that specific alignments were intentional — but what each is aligned toward varies, and determining whether those individual orientations form a larger system or represent genuinely independent choices is one of the open problems in Arbour's Installation scholarship. The scholars working on it are three people in the Tabularium's most restricted section, and they have not shared findings with each other in several years, for reasons the archive's access structure makes structurally likely without anyone choosing it.

## The Four Subtypes

### Reliquary

Memorial or ritual sites. The most common subtype by count of confirmed identifications, and the one Arbour's scholarly tradition has engaged with most extensively, partly because reliquaries tend toward smaller scale and fixed, non-radiating Aetheris profiles that make them easier to approach.

What makes a site a reliquary is not a single identifying feature but a convergence of characteristics: Latensite inscription density above the norm for other subtypes, spatial organisation consistent with ceremonial gathering rather than habitation or operation, specific material treatments that appear to mark a space as set apart from function. The Wayfarer term for what happens in reliquary spaces — not their term for the space itself, but for what the space is doing — has no direct Arbour translation and has been rendered in scholarship as approximately *held memory*, which is acknowledged to be inadequate.

Reliquaries do not generate significant Aetheris. They hold what they were given. Active reliquaries feel different from dormant ones — the texture Aran would call the Hum is present, low, steady, not the spike of a wellspring or the wrongness of collapsed containment. The specific quality of being in an active reliquary is one of the better-documented experiential accounts in the Tabularium's restricted archive, because Custodians have spent operational time in them and some of them filed unusually detailed reports.

### Observatory

Sites oriented toward the sky — toward stars, toward the binary stellar dynamics of KOI-8565, toward something the Penumbrans were tracking with precision across what must have been generations of continuous observation. The term is the Arbour scholarly default, not a translation of anything Penumbran.

Observatories tend toward elevation. Where the terrain allows it, they are built high. Where it does not, the Installation's geometry creates internal sightlines that compensate. The alignment specificity of observatories is the highest of any confirmed subtype — the intentionality of their orientation is unmistakable even to someone without the tools to determine what they were oriented toward.

What the Penumbrans were observing is not established. The obvious candidate — astronomical tracking, navigational reference, the binary stellar system's behaviour — is the least interesting possibility and probably not the complete answer. A civilisation that understood Aetheris well enough to build wellspring Installations was tracking something in the sky that Arbour's scholarship is not currently equipped to identify. The observatories are oriented toward it with consistent precision. Whatever it is, it was important enough to build to.

Aetheris in observatories is present but directional — not radiating outward but oriented inward, toward the sightlines the structure establishes. Standing in the focal point of an observatory produces a specific and distinctive Aetheris experience that several Custodians have reported and none have successfully described in programme terminology. The programme classifies it as a high-value controlled exposure environment. This is accurate and insufficient.

### Habitation Site

Structures the Penumbrans lived in, or lived near, or used in ways that are consistent with sustained occupation. The most heterogeneous subtype — habitation sites range from what appear to be individual dwellings to large complex structures that could have housed significant populations, and the distinction between a large habitation site and a small settlement is not always clear from what survives.

What is consistent: habitation sites are built around the Aetheris field rather than at its edge. The Penumbrans did not live at a distance from active Aetheris and make occasional visits to their observatories and wellsprings. They lived within the field. Their habitation sites are positioned in ways that imply the ambient Aetheris level was not a hazard to be managed but an environmental condition to be present in. Whatever the Penumbrans' biology was, it did not respond to Aetheris the way human biology responds to sustained exposure.

This is the most legible implication of the habitation sites for understanding the Penumbrans as a civilisation, and it is the one Arbour's institutions find most uncomfortable to sit with — because it implies that what Aetheris does to human nervous systems is not what it did to Penumbran nervous systems, which implies that the Penumbrans and humans are not comparable in ways that have remained unexamined for three centuries.

Habitation sites tend to be low Aetheris now. The occupation that sustained the ambient field is gone. What remains is the architecture.

### Wellspring

The most operationally significant subtype for Arbour's institutions, and the least publicly discussed. Structures built to generate, store, or channel Aetheris directly — the Penumbrans' closest functional equivalent to a power plant, though whether the mechanism is generation, storage, or channelling (and in what proportions across different sites) remains uncharacterised.

Wellsprings are identifiable by their Aetheris output: active wellsprings radiate outward, producing the hot zones documented in the exposure geography document. The outer-system Installation encountered by ARBOUR|05's survey crew was a wellspring — the structure that registered as "anomalous energy in a system with nothing artificial" was radiating in exactly the way a wellspring does, and what the integration team crudely harvested was wellspring output rather than wellspring material. This distinction matters: the crew took energy, not structure, which is why the outer-system Installation still exists and why its effects on Wei were exposure effects rather than extraction effects.

Wellsprings in the Badlands are the sites Scarlet Branch has most carefully claimed and operationally designated. The Custodian programme uses them for controlled induction exposure. The Aetheris they produce is real, measurable, and consistent in a way that makes them reliable as programme assets. Voss's operational relationship to wellsprings is more intimate than her relationship to any other Installation subtype — she has stood in the field of several and understands, at an experiential level, what they do to a human nervous system. She does not know what they were doing before humans arrived to stand in them.

The Frames site's subsurface Installation is wellspring-adjacent — not a full active wellspring, but a structure with wellspring characteristics operating at low output. Whether this is a damaged wellspring, a dormant one, or a distinct subtype that approaches wellspring function without fully instantiating it is not established.

## States: Active, Dormant, Collapsed

Every Installation is in one of three functional states, and state is the single most important variable for practical purposes.

**Active** Installations are generating, storing, or responding to Aetheris at a level that produces measurable effects in their immediate environment. Wellsprings are most often active. Reliquaries can be active at a lower level. What activates an Installation — whether it has been continuously active since the Penumbrans, whether certain conditions trigger activation in dormant sites, whether the 340% post-crash Aetheris increase reactivated sites that had been dormant for millennia — is not known.

**Dormant** Installations retain their structural integrity but produce little or no measurable Aetheris output. They can be entered, studied, and observed without the exposure risks of active sites. Whether a dormant Installation is permanently inactive or conditionally dormant is one of the questions the Custodian programme's operational data is genuinely inconclusive on. Several sites have shifted from dormant to active within the programme's two-century operational record. None have shifted from active to dormant.

**Collapsed** Installations have undergone structural failure that has disrupted whatever containment or channelling the structure provided. Collapsed sites are the most dangerous and the most unpredictable. The Aetheris they release is not directional or consistent — it disperses in patterns that don't follow the exposure model of active sites, producing the anomalous reactive effects noted in the exposure geography document. Badlands communities have the strongest practical avoidance knowledge around collapsed sites, accumulated through generations of learning what happens when you get too close to something that has broken in this specific way.

The post-crash Aetheris intensification is partly attributable to sites that were dormant before the 4.3-kiloton impact detonation and its reality-tear interaction activating or destabilising. The exposure geography has shifted since the founding generation mapped it, and the mapping from then is unreliable as a guide to conditions now.

## What Different Communities Know

**Arbour's institutions (official):** The category term, the subtype framework, and a theoretical understanding that does not extend to the causal mechanisms underneath it. The Tabularium holds three centuries of scholarship, most of it inaccessible to anyone below a specific clearance level, and most of the cleared scholarship either wrong about mechanisms or right about observations without being able to explain them. The Azure Branch's orientation toward ritual rather than understanding applies here: there are correct procedures for approaching certain Installation types, and the procedures are followed precisely without anyone currently understanding why they were written.

**Scarlet Branch (operational):** More empirical knowledge of wellspring behaviour than any other Arbour institution, accumulated through the Custodian programme's direct operational use of specific sites. This knowledge is not theoretical. It is the knowledge of people who have stood in these environments repeatedly and tracked what happens. It is also siloed — the programme does not share what it knows with the Tabularium, and the Tabularium's scholars do not know the programme's operational data exists.

**Wayfarer communities:** Practical avoidance knowledge, oral and generational. They know which structures produce which effects in which kinds of people; they do not know the Installation typology or the Aetheris mechanics. Aeolian Wayfarers have the most extensive practical knowledge by virtue of the most territory covered. Thessaly tradition treats Installations as categorically taboo — not dangerous in a practical sense but spiritually prohibited, which produces behaviour that is functionally similar to the Aeolian avoidance approach while being motivated by something else entirely. Corvane tradition treats them as landmarks in a navigational and relational sense — they appear in route descriptions, in meeting-point designations, in trade-contact references, without ever being the subject of direct engagement.

**Badlands communities (non-Wayfarer):** The most granular practical knowledge of specific local sites, accumulated across generations of proximity. Often more accurate about individual site conditions than anything in the Tabularium. Completely untheorised — the people who know that the structure in the northern valley produces specific effects in some individuals and not others do not know why, have no framework for wellspring vs. reliquary, and have no institutional structure through which their knowledge could be shared, consolidated, or built on. It exists as accumulated caution, transmitted person to person, and is lost whenever a community disperses.

**Cassan Vale:** The single individual with the widest cross-institutional knowledge base — Custodian operational data through Scarlet's reporting line, Tabularium restricted scholarship through his Twelve position, and his own direct research through the Frames facility. His understanding of what Installations are doing, at a mechanistic level, exceeds anyone else's in Arbour. He also knows how much he still doesn't understand. The distance between what he knows and what the Penumbrans knew is one of the things that drives him.

## The Unknowability Problem

The resonant layer of Penumbran language is built into the Installations. The geometry, the acoustics, the material behaviour — these are not separate from the grammar. They *are* the grammar, or part of it. An Installation that could be fully read would be a sentence. Nothing in Arbour currently has the cognitive and perceptual framework to read what is written in that register.

This is not a decipherment problem in the way the Latensite corpus is a decipherment problem. The Latensite corpus is small but theoretically solvable — given enough material, parallel text, and time, it might yield. The resonant layer does not yield to that approach because the relevant corpus is not text. It is the experience of standing in a specific geometry, with specific acoustic properties, in a specific Aetheris field, and perceiving what the combination of those things is doing. A human nervous system, parsing that experience through human perceptual apparatus, receives something. What it receives is partial, distorted, and mediated through biology the Penumbrans did not share.

Wren can describe what a reliquary *does* to someone standing in it with scholarly precision. Wren cannot read what it is *saying*.

This remains true even as Aran's Aetheris affliction deepens. What Stage Three exposure gives him is not the Penumbrans' perceptual framework — it is a human nervous system running hotter, extended past its ordinary range. He perceives more of what the Installations are producing. He does not perceive it as the Penumbrans perceived it. The visions are not translations. They are something else.
