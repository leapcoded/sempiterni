# Distributed Knowledge

*Lives in: World & Lore → The Convergence & Cosmology. Companion to The Convergence & Aetheris (mechanics), The Installations (structures), The Penumbrans / The First-Walked (civilisation overview), and Aetheris Distribution and Exposure Geography (geographic spread). This document does not repeat the community-specific Installation knowledge from The Installations → What Different Communities Know; it covers the broader cosmological picture — the Convergence, Aetheris, and the Penumbrans as a whole — and what each community holds, misreads, or cannot access.*

---

## The Problem

The truth of what Cordis is — what the Convergence is, what Aetheris is doing, who the Penumbrans were and what they understood — is not held anywhere in full. It is distributed: fragmented across institutions, communities, and traditions that do not speak to each other, that actively distrust each other's methods, and that are in several cases structurally prevented from sharing what they know.

This fragmentation is not a designed outcome. The Council's suppression apparatus maintains some of it deliberately, but most of it is the product of three centuries of institutional siloing, geographic separation, knowledge degradation, and the fundamental problem that certain kinds of knowledge do not transfer cleanly between the frameworks available to receive them. What a Badlands elder knows about a specific site's effects is not legible to a Tabularium scholar in a form the scholar can use. What the Custodian programme knows about wellspring exposure is not available to anyone studying the exposure maps. What The Margin knows about suppressed history is built on a corrupted source. What the Wayfarers know is oral, contextual, and inseparable from the traditions in which it is embedded.

The result is that no single entity on Cordis currently holds a picture of the whole that is substantially accurate. The closest approaches are two: Cassan Vale, who has assembled the widest individual knowledge base through access to multiple siloed bodies of information, and still misses crucial elements; and the assembled weight of the Wayfarer tradition, which has the most accurate phenomenological relationship to what Aetheris actually does without having a framework to theorise it.

This document maps what each community holds. A final section describes what would become visible if the fragments were assembled — not the complete truth, but the shape of it.

---

## The Convergence

The Convergence is the entity for which almost no community has an accurate name or concept.

**Arbour's official position** does not acknowledge the Convergence as a distinct phenomenon. Aetheris exists, CSD and CRS are clinical diagnoses, and the Installations are Penumbran artefacts with measurable properties. That these three phenomena share a single source — that they are all aspects of the same non-human reality bleeding through into the Penumbran Reach — is not part of any officially sanctioned framework. The closest the official record comes is in the Tabularium's most restricted scholarship, where certain researchers have proposed that Aetheris is not a property of Cordis but something introduced from outside it. This proposal is classified, contested within the small group that has access to it, and has never been connected to the Installation material or the Penumbran historical record by anyone with access to all three.

**The Twelve** know more than the official position contains. They know that Aetheris is not curable and not merely a biological disorder. They know that the Installations are not inert ruins. They do not have a unified framework for what the Convergence is — the knowledge inside the Twelve is itself compartmentalised, and the picture Cassan holds is not the same picture anyone else on the Twelve holds.

**Wayfarer tradition** — a broad culture with more branches than are relevant to Book One; the three that bear on the narrative are Aeolian, Thessaly, and Corvane — engages with the Convergence's presence without naming it as such, and each branch's approach reflects its own relationship to the landscape. The Aeolian branch names something *the pull beneath things*: a quality of the world that is not wind, not weather, not the behaviour of any living thing, but that is nonetheless present and has direction. This is not theorised. It is described, across generations of oral account, as something that can be read in the way weather can be read: not understood mechanically, but felt accurately enough to act on. The Aeolian tradition has never concluded that *the pull beneath things* is a single entity. It has concluded that it is consistent enough to warrant attention. This is, arguably, the most accurate available description of the Convergence that exists in current circulation — and it is known to no one outside Aeolian tradition.

Thessaly does not name the Convergence-as-entity either, but its categorical taboo around Installations implies an awareness of something present in those structures that should not be engaged with. The taboo functions as prohibition rather than description — what is being avoided is not named, only marked. Corvane's navigational relationship to the landscape includes a working knowledge that certain territories behave differently, which functions as practical caution without rising to cosmological claim. All three branches are living alongside the same underlying reality; the difference is in what each tradition built around it.

**Badlands communities** (non-Wayfarer) have the most ground-level exposure to Aetheris effects and the least theoretical framework around what produces them. The Convergence as a concept is entirely absent from their self-understanding. What they have instead is accumulated, specific knowledge about specific places and specific effects, transmitted as caution and practice. This knowledge is accurate at the local level and unconnected to any wider picture.

**The Margin** does not have a position on the Convergence because The Margin does not know it needs one. Its foundational document concerns historical suppression and institutional cover-up. The cosmological dimension of what Arbour is built on is outside its field of inquiry.

## Aetheris

Aetheris is the most widely known phenomenon and the one with the most divergent interpretations.

**Arbour's official position** is CSD and CRS: a clinical, progressive disorder managed through suppressants and quarantine. The institutional line holds that Aetheris exposure is a biological contamination, that its progression is linear and inevitable without intervention, and that quarantine protects both the individual and the community. None of the three load-bearing claims in this position are accurate — exposure outcome is stochastic rather than linear, progression is not inevitable, and quarantine serves the Council's control function rather than any coherent public health rationale. The clinicians who administer the programme believe it. The researchers who developed the diagnostic framework believed it. The corruption entered through the top, not the practitioners.

**Scarlet Branch** knows the official position is inadequate without knowing precisely how. The Custodian programme's operational experience with Stage Two and Three individuals who are functional, stable, and in some cases enhanced by their exposure is direct empirical counter-evidence to the inevitability claim — and Scarlet holds this evidence siloed, because acknowledging it would destabilise the quarantine mandate that Scarlet's institutional position depends on. Voss knows the progression is not linear. She does not have a framework that explains why, and the programme does not encourage her to develop one.

**Cassan Vale** has the most mechanistically accurate understanding of Aetheris available to any living person. He knows it is not a disorder but a process — a genuine biological interaction with a non-human physical substrate. He knows it can be directed, in limited ways, under controlled conditions. He knows the stages are real but the Council's interpretation of them is wrong. What he does not know: what the process is *for*, from the Convergence's perspective — whether "for" is even a meaningful question to ask about something that does not have intent in any human sense. His research is aimed at mastery and has produced real insight. It has produced insight about a mechanism he is still fundamentally misunderstanding at the causal level.

**Wayfarer communities** understand Aetheris phenomenologically — what it does to people, how it presents, what it feels like from inside the experience, and which approaches to the landscape produce which effects. Wayfarer culture encompasses more branches than are directly relevant to the narrative; of the three that bear on Book One, each arrives at this understanding differently. The Aeolian branch has the most developed vocabulary, accumulated over generations of scouts and travellers who worked in high-exposure territory; the vocabulary is not scientific but is the product of a community that has learned to read a terrain feature the way it reads weather, with corresponding practices — routes, timings, individual monitoring, community response — that work. Thessaly's taboo framework produces functionally similar avoidance behaviour through entirely different means: the practice is correct without the reasoning underneath it being the same. Corvane's navigational tradition encodes Aetheris-affected territory as a landscape characteristic — present in route descriptions and meeting-point designations, treated as a feature to work around rather than a phenomenon to name. Across all three branches, the framework that would explain *why* the practices work is absent. This does not make them less effective.

**The Badlands communities** understand Aetheris as a property of places, not of a system. The structure in the northern valley does this to some people. The collapsed site by the dry riverbed is to be avoided entirely. The active wellspring on the plateau produces a specific kind of headache in most people and something more serious in a few. This knowledge is accurate, site-specific, and totally untheorised. It is also the most granular first-contact knowledge available and is lost whenever a community disperses — lost from the record, not from the land.

## The Penumbrans

Knowledge of the Penumbrans is, in most communities, indirect — present as artefact and as effect rather than as history.

**Arbour's scholarly tradition** has three centuries of record. The record is extensive, partially accurate, and shaped at its foundations by the conditions under which it was compiled: scholars working from structures they could not enter safely, with an Aetheris framework that prevented them from engaging with the material as the Penumbrans intended it to be engaged with. The resulting scholarship knows a great deal about what Installations look like and very little about what they were doing. It knows the Penumbrans existed, that they were technologically sophisticated, and that they are gone. Why they are gone — whether the Convergence consumed them, whether they chose something, whether the transformation was catastrophic or directed — is not in the record.

**Wayfarer tradition** knows the Penumbrans as *the First-Walked*: the people who came before, who shaped the landscape in ways that persist, whose traces are to be respected and in some traditions avoided. The name and the general understanding are shared across Wayfarer culture broadly; of the three branches relevant to the narrative, what each has built around that shared understanding differs. The Aeolian branch holds the most extensive oral record — not history in a Tabularium sense, but accumulated account, image, and practice that preserves aspects of what living alongside the Penumbrans' landscape means. Some of this oral record preserves specific observations that the scholarly tradition has independently arrived at, through different means, centuries later. Some of it preserves things the scholarly tradition has not arrived at and would not know how to use if it did. Thessaly's relationship to the First-Walked is shaped by the taboo tradition — reverence through avoidance rather than through engagement. Corvane carries the knowledge of specific sites as waypoints, which means it holds locational precision about First-Walked structures that neither Aeolian oral history nor Tabularium cartography has in the same form.

**Badlands communities** live closest to what the Penumbrans left. They call them by various names across different communities — not a unified term, but names that cluster around ideas of age, prior presence, and making. Several Badlands communities have practices around specific sites that are older than the community's memory of originating them — practices that appear to have been inherited from contact with previous communities, and possibly from contact with a period when the sites themselves were active in ways they no longer are. This is the most archaeologically direct relationship with the Penumbrans available, and it is held in communities with no institutional presence and no means of external transmission.

**The Margin's** foundational document holds a Penumbran reference: it attributes the crash to the founding crew's discovery of something the Penumbrans left and a decision to exploit it. This is closer to what happened than the Council's version and further from it than The Margin knows. The outer-system wellspring was real. The crew's interaction with it was real. The claim that the crash was deliberate sabotage — that human agency decided the fate of ARBOUR|05 — is the error. The Convergence's role is not in the document.

**Cassan Vale** has engaged with Penumbran material more directly and more deliberately than any other living researcher. His understanding of Installation mechanics exceeds the Tabularium's official scholarship. He knows, at a structural level, that the Penumbrans were not simply a predecessor civilisation but a people who had a working relationship with the Convergence — that what they built was not independent of what Aetheris is, but an expression of it, directed by their understanding of it. He does not know what that understanding was. The distance between what he knows and what the Penumbrans knew is one of the things that drives him, and one of the things he will never close by the methods available to him.

## The Penumbrans' Own Knowledge

The Penumbrans understood the Convergence in ways that no current community can access. Their Installations are not just artefacts — they are the record of that understanding, written in a language that requires both the perceptual framework and the biological relationship to the Aetheris field that the Penumbrans had and humans do not.

This gap is not a puzzle waiting for the right key. It is not a decipherment problem. The resonant layer of Penumbran language is built into the structures in a way that requires the receiver to be changed in specific ways to receive anything from it, and even a maximally changed human nervous system — Aran at his furthest Stage Three — receives something partial, distorted, and mediated. What the Penumbrans knew about the Convergence, what they decided about it, and what happened to them as a result: this layer is closed. The shape of the gap is legible. The content of what sits in it is not.

## What the Assembled Fragments Would Reveal

No single community can see this. It requires holding all of the above simultaneously, which is not a thing any institution or individual on Cordis currently does.

**The Convergence is not Cordis's problem specifically.** It is a non-human physical reality whose boundary with the Penumbran Reach is the relevant fact, and the Penumbran Reach is a location, not a destination. The crash brought ARBOUR|05 into it. The fragments — Aeolian phenomenological description, Cassan's mechanistic knowledge, the Badlands site-specific record, Scarlet's operational data — all point toward this without any one of them stating it.

**The exposure outcome is not a function of exposure alone.** The stochastic element that the official framework erases is real and significant, and multiple fragments confirm it: Scarlet's operational evidence of stable Stage Two and Three individuals, the Badlands communities' knowledge that certain people are affected differently at the same sites, the Aeolian tradition's practice of individual monitoring rather than blanket avoidance. The framework that would connect these three independent confirmations does not exist. Nobody is in a position to build it.

**The Installations are not ruins.** The dormant-to-active shift data Scarlet holds, the acoustic and Aetheris properties that Tabularium scholarship has documented without understanding, the Badlands communities' knowledge that certain sites behave differently at different times, and the Wayfarer tradition's awareness that *the pull beneath things* is not static — all of this points toward structures that are in some sense still operating, still oriented toward whatever they were built to do, still waiting on conditions the Penumbrans understood and left no legible instructions for. What they are waiting for is not in any fragment. What happens when the conditions arrive is not in any fragment.

**The crash was not incidental.** The Margin is wrong about agency — it was not sabotage. Cassan is right about mechanism — the outer-system wellspring and Wei's integration produced something that propagated. What neither holds is the third layer: the 340% Aetheris intensification on impact, the reactivation of dormant sites, the slow intensification across three centuries since, and the Convergence's apparent selectivity in who and what it draws toward full Stage Three — all of this, assembled, has the shape of a process rather than an event. Not a plan, because the Convergence does not plan. A physics. Something that, once started, runs to completion unless something stops it. Whether anything can stop it is the question that no fragment currently addresses, because no fragment is large enough to have identified that it is a question.
